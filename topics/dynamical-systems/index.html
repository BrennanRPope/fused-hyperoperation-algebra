The circle operation squaring is z² in other coordinates, and folding it by the circle-operation inverse gives the logistic map

The lift z = 1 + x is a change of coordinates, and in dynamics a change of coordinates (a conjugacy) keeps everything that matters: fixed points, how hard they pull, chaos. So x ∘₁ x = (1 + x)² − 1 behaves exactly like z ↦ z². The wall −1 is the point that swallows everything inside the circle |1 + x| = 1. Fold the plane by pairing each w with its circle-operation inverse and the circle collapses onto [0, 1], where the same orbit becomes the chaotic logistic map 4X(1 − X).

The wall pulls hardest; the identity pushes

Near the wall, (−1 + h) ∘₁ (−1 + h) = −1 + h². A small offset is squared, so the wall is superattracting (multiplier 0): that is the absorbing −1 seen as a place orbits fall into. Near the identity, h ∘₁ h = 2h + h², so 0 repels with multiplier 2. The boundary between falling in and escaping is the circle |1 + x| = 1 (the Julia set), and on it the map doubles the angle: chaos with Lyapunov exponent log 2.

The dial changes nothing essential. k·(x ∘ₖ x) = (kx) ∘₁ (kx), so every k ≠ 0 is the same map in other units, with its wall −1/k still superattracting. At k = −2 (parity) the square is 2x(1 − x): the logistic map at r = 2, whose famous superstable point 1/2 is the parity wall.

Two rulers: one stretches, one shifts

A repelling or attracting fixed point can be straightened. Koenigs (1884): there is a coordinate in which the map is plain multiplication by its multiplier. For x ∘₁ x at the identity that coordinate is log(1 + x), the project's log ruler: log(1 + x ∘₁ x) = 2·log(1 + x). It is the same ruler that turns ∘₁ into +, and Koenigs's own limit 2ⁿ·f⁻ⁿ(x) recovers it.

The rung map E(x) = e^x − 1 has multiplier 1 at 0, and no stretching coordinate exists (the equation fails at the x² term). What exists instead is Fatou's coordinate, a ruler on which the map is a shift by 1: α(E(x)) = α(x) + 1, with α = −2/x + ⅓·log|x| − x/36 + …. That is the project's "ruler for which rung you're on", and half a step along it gives E^(1/2)(1) = 1.27103, the fractional-rungs value.

The rung flow is the flow of one vector field, and 0 is its parabolic point

Fractional rungs E^t are the flow of v = 1/α′ = x²/2 − x³/12 + x⁴/48 − x⁵/180 + 11x⁶/8640 − …, and E is its time-1 map: v(E(x)) = E′(x)·v(x). v is positive on both sides of 0, so the flow pushes everything to the right: negative numbers creep up to 0 forever, positive ones leave and then explode.

Near 0 the flow is the Möbius flow x/(1 − t·x/2) of x′ = x²/2, whose ruler −2/x adds t exactly. So locally the rung ladder is a landscape-style Möbius map, and its two walls have merged at 0 (a double wall, the parabola case). The landscape itself is a family of flows too: each law F_{k,d} is "run y′ = 1 + ky + dy² for time a, then time b", and the circle operation is y′ = 1 + y, whose equilibrium is the wall.

The ruler from the left and the ruler from the right disagree by a tiny wobble

The Abel ruler can be built from the right (pulling x down to 0 with log(1 + x)) or from the left (pushing x up with e^x − 1). On the real line they cover different halves. In the complex plane they overlap above and below 0, and there they differ by α₊ − α₋ = c₁·e^(2πiα) + …: a periodic wobble that shrinks like e^(−2π·Im α).

We measured c₁ ≈ −10.749 − 63.246i (|c₁| ≈ 64.15, with the ruler normalized to have no constant term), the same to seven digits at four points. A nonzero c₁ is Écalle and Voronin's invariant saying that e^x − 1 has no analytic flow through 0: the fractional rungs on the two sides are two different families that cannot be joined smoothly in the complex plane. That is Baker's 1958 divergence result, seen as a number.

The logistic fold, Chebyshev and the tent map are one picture

X(w) = w·ι(w)/4 = −w²/(4(1 + w)),  ι(w) = −w/(1 + w)

Because w ∘₁ ι(w) = 0, the sum and product of w and its inverse are negatives of each other, so X = −(w + ι(w))/4. X forgets which of the pair you started from (2-to-1), squaring commutes with ι, and so 4X(1 − X) = X(w ∘₁ w). On the circle w = e^(iθ) − 1, X = sin²(θ/2): uniform angles fold into the logistic map's density 1/(π√(X(1 − X))), and its Lyapunov exponent is log 2.

In u = 1 − 2X the logistic step is the Chebyshev polynomial T₂, and every Tₙ is zⁿ seen through Joukowski's map (z + 1/z)/2 = 1 + (w + ι(w))/2. Fold the angle-doubling circle by θ ↔ −θ and you get the tent map. The chain rule reads the same way: slopes multiply along an orbit, so slope − 1 composes by ∘₁, a relabelling of the product.

Lead worth following?

Yes, a modest one: the horn map of the rung flow. Everything else on this page is classical dynamics seen through the lift, and the lift being a conjugacy is exactly how dynamicists already think. The useful output there is a dictionary (wall = superattracting point, identity = repelling point with ruler log(1 + x), circle-operation inverse = the fold that makes the logistic map), not a discovery.

The horn map is different. It measures, in one number per frequency, how the project's fractional rungs fail to be one family across 0. Computing c₂, c₃, … and checking them against the Borel-plane route of Dudko and Sauzin would turn "the rung ruler" into a fully described object. We found no genuine connection to Feigenbaum's period doubling, so it is left out.
