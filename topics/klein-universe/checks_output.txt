== U1. The Klein space E7 and the half-turn space E2, exactly
PASS U1 (exact rational arithmetic over every group element up to word length 6)  | E7 (Klein space, generators: slide a, slide b, glide = mirror x->-x then slide c): 377 elements, none with a fixed point, 182 reverse orientation (each slides an odd multiple of c), and the orientation-keeping ones are exactly the slides of the lattice (a, b, 2c), so the orientable double cover is a 3-torus. E2 (half-turn space): 377 elements, none with a fixed point, 0 reverse orientation. Both spaces are flat: every gluing map is a rigid motion, so curvature and torsion are zero at every point; the twist is only the holonomy of a loop (det -1 for the glide, +1 for the half-turn)

== U3. How small a Klein-shaped universe can still be
PASS U3 (exact formula, checked by brute force over the group; bound from Vaudrevange et al. 2012 and COMPACT Part I)  | nearest copy of us in E7 = min(a, b, 2c, sqrt(4 d^2 + c^2)) with d our distance from the nearest mirror plane (worst mismatch against brute force over 25 random universes and positions: 1.1e-16). The circle bound needs every term above 0.985 D. So the shortest loop of a Klein-shaped universe (the glide, length c, through a mirror plane) can be as short as 0.4925 D = 13.7 Gpc, about half the last-scattering diameter, if we sit at least sqrt(0.985^2 - c^2)/2 from every mirror plane; at that shortest c this needs a >= 1.706 D = 47.3 Gpc. Example: a = 2 D, c = 0.6 D leaves 22% of positions allowed. COMPACT Part IIb: such a universe's correlations could beat cosmic variance if the nearest copy is under about 1.2 D = 33.3 Gpc, so the window 0.985 D to 1.2 D (27.3 to 33.3 Gpc) is allowed today and testable
== U4. Handedness across an orientation-reversing loop
PASS U4 (exact sympy for the spinor algebra; measured CKM numbers, PDG 2024)  | for each mirror x_i -> -x_i the spinor map S = gamma_i gamma5 represents it exactly (S^-1 gamma^mu S = Lambda gamma), and S gamma5 S^-1 = -gamma5: a left-handed field carried round the glide loop comes back right-handed. Every rotation (the half-turn included) commutes with gamma5 and keeps the hand. So a Klein-shaped space needs laws that are unchanged by a mirror (with the CRT theorem, the needed symmetry is CR, equivalent to T). The weak force breaks P outright and the Standard Model breaks CP: Jarlskog invariant J = 3.116e-05 from the PDG Wolfenstein parameters (unitarity error 7e-18); kaon epsilon_K = 2.228e-3 (first seen 1964)
== U5. If a field chose the hand, a Klein-shaped universe would need a mirror wall
PASS U5 (numbers, order of magnitude; Zel'dovich, Kobzarev and Okun 1974)  | going round the glide must turn the field's choice into its mirror, so it has to flip somewhere: a wall. Critical density 3.67e-47 GeV^4. A horizon-sized wall stays under it only if its tension is below (29 MeV)^3, and keeps the CMB smooth to 1e-5 only below (0.70 MeV)^3. A hand chosen at the weak scale or above gives tension about v^3 = 1.5e+07 GeV^3, 4e+16 times too heavy. The wall could hide only beyond our horizon, and then the twist would be out of sight too
== U6. A twist of the shape is not torsion
PASS U6 (exact for the shape, order of magnitude for Einstein-Cartan; Hehl et al. 1976, Kostelecky, Russell and Tasson 2008)  | the shape's twist: zero curvature and zero torsion at every point (U1); it shows only in the holonomy of a loop that goes all the way round the universe (det -1 for the Klein glide, +1 for the half-turn). Local torsion (Einstein-Cartan) is a field set by spin density at each point and zero in empty space; it matters only near the Cartan density m/(lambda_C l_P^2) = 3.1e+58 kg/m^3 for neutrons, 3e+40 times a neutron star's core; lab tests bound background torsion near 1e-31 GeV. == U7. What going round a twist does to a spinor's phase
PASS U7 (exact sympy; Pin structures as in Kirby and Taylor 1990, Berg, DeWitt-Morette, Gwo and Kriele 2001)  | on Klein space a spinor can't be a plain (Spin) spinor; it must be a Pin spinor, and there are two kinds, set by whether the mirror's spinor map squares to +1 (Pin+) or -1 (Pin-). Both maps represent the same mirror; they differ only by a phase i. Each kind has 8 versions on E7 (one free sign for each of the slide a, slide b and the glide; every one satisfies the gluing rules), matching H^1(E7; Z2) = (Z2)^3. Going once round the glide multiplies a spinor by the mirror map: eigenvalues ['-1', '1'] for Pin+ (a sign) and ['-I', 'I'] for Pin- (a quarter turn of phase). Going twice round, which is once round the long slide 2c, gives 1 for Pin+ and -1 for Pin-, forced, with no choice left: a half turn of phase. For contrast the half-turn space's screw lifts to gamma1 gamma2, eigenvalues ['-I', 'I'], and twice round gives -1, the usual sign of a 360-degree turn. None of this touches time: the glide moves only space, so cosmic time stays one single-valued clock on a Klein-shaped universe
