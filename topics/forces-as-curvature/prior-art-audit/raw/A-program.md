# Prior-art check A: the one-law, curvature-dial physics program (claims A1–A6)

Checked 2026-11-27. Sources were read through web search and page fetches (arXiv abstract and PDF pages, journal pages). Where I only saw a secondary citation, the table says so.

**Headline.** The core of the program is already in print: the same quaternion law with a curvature parameter, used for the sphere, for de Sitter with an indefinite norm, and for FRW slices at K = +1, 0, −1, with curvature read from the law's associator (holonomy). That work is by **Nesterov & Sabinin (2000–2004)**. The 3D formula itself also appears letter for letter as κ-addition in the machine-learning literature (Bachmann et al. 2020; Skopek et al. 2020). What the project adds is its particular packaging and its numerical checks (Regge slabs, grown 600-cell corners, the Hopf/photon reading, the "fused shift" reading of time). None of these is a new physical result or a new prediction.

## A1: the closest prior program

The closest neighbour is **Nesterov & Sabinin's "non-associative geometry"** (CMUC 2000; PRD 2000; hep-th/0406229; arXiv:1304.7763), built on Sabinin's smooth loops (*Smooth Quasigroups and Loops*, Kluwer 1999) and on Kikkawa's homogeneous Lie loops (*Hiroshima Math. J.* 5, 1975). It has almost the same skeleton:
- one quaternionic Möbius-type loop with a curvature constant K;
- spherical, flat and hyperbolic slices from the sign of K;
- de Sitter from the same law with an indefinite norm;
- curvature recovered from the associator as triangle holonomy;
- discrete lattice versions;
- a later "loop of boosts" giving Thomas precession.

Ungar's gyrovector program supplies the q<0 relativity side. It is hyperbolic only; the papers I opened say its spherical side was not developed before the ML papers. Kiechle (*Theory of K-loops*, LNM 1778, 2002) has a chapter on relativistic velocity addition. The κ-stereographic papers supply the identical formula across signs, but with no physics. Relative locality is a different program: its composition law acts on momentum space, not spacetime.

As far as this search shows, three things in the project's packaging appear in none of them:
1. tying the 3D law to the scalar circle law a+b+kab and its "fused shift";
2. building Regge slabs from law-grown corners (the 600-cell) and checking Einstein's equations with curvature q;
3. reading the q=+1 twist as the Hopf connection and the lowest photon on S³.

These are reconstructions of known physics, so they do not open a new physical lead. None yields a testable prediction. Of the neighbours, only relative locality / DSR makes testable claims (e.g. energy-dependent photon arrival times), and the project does not currently connect to them.
