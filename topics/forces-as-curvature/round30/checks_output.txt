== L1. A round lump's slice at rest is the law in every plane, with a dial that depends on direction

== L2. Fitting (the Bianchi identity) plus the law's local rule builds the tide
PASS L2 (exact + numbers) the contracted Bianchi identity, which every geometry obeys whatever the matter, holds identically here and says qbar' = -(2/r)(across - along): wherever the across-dial changes with radius, the along-dial must split from it. Add the law's local rule (mean bending = q_loc = q + 8 pi rho/3) and it becomes (r^3 qbar)' = 3 r^2 q_loc, so qbar is q_loc averaged over the ball inside, and the tide follows. == L3. Without round symmetry: what the law's rule and fitting leave free is a gravitational wave
PASS L3 (exact, linearised; textbook York 1973) away from round symmetry a slice's shape has 6 functions; 3 are only the choice of coordinates and the law's local rule fixes 1, leaving 2 per point. A transverse ripple in those 2 (both polarisations, A and B) keeps every local dial at its law value (scalar curvature unchanged) yet bends planes differently by direction: it is a tide carried by a gravitational wave (round 26).
