== C1. The law needs no algebra: it is a rotation in the plane of 1 and x
PASS C1 (numbers; textbook: the sphere as a symmetric space, its transvections) the law x (+) y = x^(1/2) y x^(1/2) is the rotation in the plane of 1 and x that carries 1 to x, applied to y, leaving every direction across that plane alone. That holds for complex numbers, quaternions and octonions. With sedenions the sandwich falls off the sphere (they don't keep lengths), but the rotation is defined on every sphere. So the law needs no multiplication at all: only the round sphere and a chosen point 1  | complex (n=2): rotation vs sandwich 6.3e-16, sandwich off sphere 0.00, rotation off sphere 4.4e-16, carries 1 to x 3.5e-14; quaternions (n=4): rotation vs sandwich 5.5e-16, sandwich off sphere 0.00, rotation off sphere 4.4e-16, carries 1 to x 4.9e-16; octonions (n=8): rotation vs sandwich 6.8e-16, sandwich off sphere 0.00, rotation off sphere 3.3e-16, carries 1 to x 3.9e-16; sedenions (n=16): rotation vs sandwich 5.8e-01, sandwich off sphere 0.36, rotation off sphere 3.3e-16, carries 1 to x 3.2e-16
== C2. The law's symmetries: every rotation of the other directions, mirrors included
PASS C2 (numbers) the rotations that respect the law are exactly those that keep 1 fixed: all of SO(n-1) on the sphere S^(n-1), with mirrors too. On round 1's 3-sphere that is SO(3). On the octonions' 7-sphere it is SO(7), 21 dimensions, not just the 14 of G2 that respect octonion multiplication: the law doesn't see the octonions. == C3. Which sphere holds colour, the weak force and hypercharge, with one-handed matter (exact)
PASS C3 (exact; textbook SO(10) unification, Georgi 1974 and Fritzsch-Minkowski 1975) so the law on a 10-sphere has the symmetry SO(10). For colour (SU(3) needs 6 directions), a commuting weak SU(2) (3 more) and one-handed matter (spinors of SO(m) come in two distinct hands only for m = 2 mod 4), 10 is the smallest. Split its 10 directions 6 + 4: the 6 carry colour and lepton number, the 4 carry the weak force, left and right. One hand of its spinor is exactly one generation: Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c and a right-handed neutrino, with the measured hypercharges, Y = T3R + (B - L)/2, fixed by the group with nothing to choose. Every anomaly cancels and the doublets come in an even number. This fills both of round 33's gaps at once: hypercharge is not the circle weight but a rotation of the 10 directions, and colour is the rotation of 6 of them  | sizes that work up to 14: [10, 14]; the 16 states: {'e^c': 1, 'nu^c': 1, 'Q': 6, 'd^c': 3, 'u^c': 3, 'L': 2} (Standard Model generation plus nu^c: {'Q': 6, 'u^c': 3, 'd^c': 3, 'L': 2, 'e^c': 1, 'nu^c': 1}); Tr Y = 0, Tr Y^3 = 0, Tr T3L^2 Y = 0, Tr T_colour^2 Y = 0; weak doublets 4 (even: no Witten anomaly); electric charges ['-1', '-1/3', '-2/3', '0', '1', '1/3', '2/3']
== C4. The weak mixing angle SO(10) fixes, run down to the Z mass
PASS C4 (exact 3/8; textbook one-loop running) SO(10) fixes the weak mixing angle where it holds: sin^2 theta_W = Tr T3^2 / Tr Q^2 = 3/8 over one generation. Run down with the Standard Model's own particles, the three couplings never meet at one point: hypercharge meets the weak force near 10^13 GeV, hypercharge meets colour near 2 x 10^14 and the weak force meets colour near 10^17. Forcing a single meeting predicts sin^2 theta_W = 0.21 at the Z mass, where 0.231 is measured. So SO(10) broken straight to the Standard Model misses; it needs something in between (in textbook models an intermediate stage such as SU(4) x SU(2) x SU(2), or new particles), and the law doesn't supply that yet.
