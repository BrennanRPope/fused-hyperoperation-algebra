The dial's potential: the Higgs reading misses, the dark-energy reading is alive

71 guesses in four rounds, each scored against measured numbers. No guess that makes the dial the Higgs field survives with a prediction of its own. The one reading still standing is the dial as a slowly rolling field today: its rescaling symmetry forces an exponential potential, and that fits supernovae and galaxy surveys better than a fixed cosmological constant, by a margin that is real but not yet decisive.

Net bits: the evidence a guess earns from the numbers it predicts, minus what it cost to pick it (one choice out of a list, and the size of the whole search). Above zero means it did better than chance after those costs. Ruled-out guesses are drawn at the floor.

How each guess was scored

A guess is a shape for the dial's potential plus what the dial couples to. Each one predicts some measured numbers. A prediction p for a measured x earns bits = log₂(W / (s√2π)) − z²/(2 ln 2), where s is the combined uncertainty (measurement plus the guess's own theory error), z = ln(p/x)/s, and W is how widely the number could have fallen beforehand (0.01 to 10 for ratios of order one). Sharp and close scores high; a miss goes very negative; more than 3σ off is ruled out.

Three guards against numerology. A number used to fix a free parameter earns nothing. Picking a constant from a list of G candidates costs log₂ G bits (215 fractions times powers of π: 7.7 bits; 450 mass sum rules: 8.8 bits), and the whole search costs log₂ of the number of guesses tried. The formula families were also run against random targets to see how often chance alone does as well. Tree-level mass relations carry a 2% theory error for loop corrections.

For the dark-energy guesses the score is Δχ² against a cosmological constant on 1,590 Pantheon+ supernovae and 13 DESI DR2 BAO numbers, converted to bits. Free parameters are fitted on one data set and scored on the other (both ways), so a guess only gains by predicting data it did not see.

Rounds 2 to 4: the closest guesses refined

Each round kept the five best-scoring guesses, plus the best near miss (within 4σ) of any other family, and made children from them: the neighbouring constants in the formula lists, the "flat dial" condition moved to other high scales, and for the rolling dial a slope step that halves each round (±0.3, ±0.15, ±0.075) plus two hump-shaped potentials. Rounds 3 and 4 had 15 and 16 children because the formula neighbourhoods ran out.

  • Formulas: a constant near the best hit is always found (mH within 1% from λ = 2/(5π); yt = 1 gives mt within 1%), and its neighbours scatter both ways. Net bits stay below −5 throughout: the closeness costs more to find than it earns.
  • Flat dial at other scales: moving the top scale from the Planck mass down to 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁷ GeV changes the Higgs mass by under 1 GeV; only the seesaw scale (10¹⁴) gets within 3σ, at 127.8 GeV, and nothing picks that scale out. Adding "level" (zero slope too) makes it worse at every scale.
  • Rolling dial: the slope walk settled between 0.74 and 0.89; the full fit's best is 0.80. A dial on a circle (negative dial, cosine potential) scores Δχ² = 3.3 with one more parameter; the literal sombrero today (rolling off the hump) does no better than a constant (Δχ² = 0.1).

The Higgs reading: closest physics is 3.3σ off, and it's known physics

If the dial is the size of the Higgs field, the flip symmetry (proved: the dial −k is the dial k seen through a ↦ −a) makes the potential even, so the dips sit at ±k0, exactly the 1D sombrero. But the dial's rescaling symmetry (also proved) means no tree-level shape can set k0 by itself. Something with units has to. The guesses that try:

  • Flat dial at the Planck scale (the hump vanishes at the top scale, where the dial returns to plain addition): λ(MPl) = 0 gives mH = 128.7 ± 1.0 GeV (NNLO fit of Buttazzo et al. 2013; our own two-loop run gives 128.6). Measured 125.2: 3.3σ. It needs the top quark 1.5 GeV lighter than measured. This is Froggatt and Nielsen's 1995 prediction (135 ± 9 GeV) and Shaposhnikov and Wetterich's 2009 one (126 GeV), redone with today's top mass.
  • Flat and level (λ = βλ = 0): mH 133.1, mt 174.8 GeV; the held-out top mass is 2.9σ off and the Higgs 8σ.
  • Scale-symmetric dial (Coleman–Weinberg, the most geometric option): mH² = (6mW⁴ + 3mZ⁴ − 12mt⁴)/(8π²v²) is negative. The top quark kills it, by a factor of 22 (proved in Lean).
  • Cutoff-blind (Veltman): 313 GeV, over twice the measured mass (proved).

The Higgs quartic run up to the Planck scale

With measured inputs λ crosses zero near 4·10¹⁰ GeV and ends at −0.010 at MPl. "Flat at the top" would need the curve to land on zero.

The dark-energy reading: the one guess that keeps earning

Rescaling the dial changes nothing (k → k/s with a → s·a), so the natural measure on the dial is dk/k and the natural field is ln k. A potential that respects the rescaling is a single power of k (proved), which in ln k is an exponential, V = V0 e−λφ. That is a thawing quintessence field: frozen early, rolling a little today.

  • Fit to all data: slope 0.80 (1σ 0.61 to 0.96), Δχ² = 4.8 better than a constant, today's w = −0.90.
  • Held out: fitted on supernovae alone it predicts the BAO numbers with χ² 8.9 against the constant's 24.1; fitted on BAO alone it predicts the supernovae 4 better. The gain does not come from fitting both at once.
  • The slope √(2/3) = 0.816 (the dial as the metric's conformal factor, V ∝ k) lands on the best fit. On its own that means little: 13 of 43 simple slopes √(p/q) sit inside the 1σ range.
  • That conformal reading has a held-out test, and fails it unscreened: the same factor couples to matter with α0² = 1/6, giving PPN γ = 5/7 (proved), 12,000σ from Cassini. It survives only with screening near masses, as f(R) gravity needs, or if the dial is dark-only, and then the slope is free.
  • It cannot be light's dial. If k = 1/c² rolled like this, α would have shifted by 9% since z = 1.15; quasar spectra allow about 10⁻⁶. The coupling to light must be below 2.4·10⁻⁵ of natural.

Leads, each judged on its own merits

Worth following: the dial as a dark-only rolling field

It is the one place where a proved symmetry of the dial fixes the form of the potential, leaving one number, and that form beats a constant on data it was not fitted to. The preference is modest (about 2σ) and is the same hint DESI reported in 2025; it leans on the supernova sample, and Pantheon+ is the gentler one. It becomes a real test when DESI's final data and Euclid pin w(z): an exponential dial predicts a specific thawing curve from one slope. Next step: refit with the other supernova samples and a CMB distance prior, and compare the dial's one-parameter curve with DESI's w0–wa result.

Known physics, now in tension: flat dial at the Planck scale

The cleanest Higgs-sector use of "the dial is plain addition at the top". It is not ours (1995, 2009), and today's top mass puts it 3.3σ off. It revives only if the top mass comes down by about 1.5 GeV, which the measurement does not allow today.

Not leads: the formula hits

mH = v/2, yt = 1 and the mass sum rule mH² + mW² + mZ² + mt² = v² each land within 1 to 2%. Random targets are matched that well 26 to 39% of the time by the same lists, none comes with a second number to hold out, and none has a geometric reason behind it.

Ruled out

Coleman–Weinberg Higgs, Veltman, one dial for all three forces (sin²θW 0.208 against 0.231), a quarter-turn mixing angle, the dial alone as gravity (half the light bending), light's dial rolling, dark energy from v²/MPl (90 times too small), 1/α = 4π³ + π² + π (misses at the tenth digit), a steep exponential (λ ≥ 1.2) and the unscreened conformal coupling.

On Brennan's mass idea: the curvature at the bottom of the dip is a mass in both readings, but only the Higgs reading makes it a number we can check, and there it is an input, not a prediction. In the dark-energy reading the dial's mass is about the Hubble rate, 10⁻³³ eV, seen only through the expansion.

Proved in Lean

24 theorems in FusedAlgebra/Topics/DialPotential, core Lean 4.37, no mathlib, no sorry, standard axioms only; merged into the shared library, which builds clean at 2,807 theorems.

  • The dial's two symmetries: flip (circK (−k) a b = −circK k (−a) (−b)) and rescaling (if k′s = k, the dial k′ on s·a, s·b is s times the dial k).
  • A potential with V(s·k) = sⁿV(k) is V(1)·kⁿ; the double well satisfies that for no n, so an outside scale must set k0.
  • The mirror well: k ✳ (−k) = −k², (k ✳ (−k) + k0²)² = (k0² − k²)², even, zero at ±k0, hump k0⁴, expansion at the dip 4k0²h² + 4k0h³ + h⁴; in the usual normalisation m² = 2λv² and hump height m²v²/8.
  • Exponential fixed point: Ω = 1 and w = λ²/3 − 1 (−7/9 and −1/9 for the conformal slopes), existing only for λ² ≤ 6.
  • Exact arithmetic on measured masses: the Coleman–Weinberg mass squared is negative (and stays so with 10 GeV shifts), the Veltman mass is over twice mH, and the conformal coupling gives γ = 5/7.
