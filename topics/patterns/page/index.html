One 2×2 step sits under every law, and Fibonacci is one setting of it

Every law on the map, the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0), velocity addition, slope addition, the channels, the Euler characteristic, is the same machine: adding a value is one 2×2 step, and adding it n times is that step taken n times. Taking a 2×2 step n times always follows one recurrence, Lucas's: U(n+2) = P·U(n+1) − Q·U(n), where P is the step's trace and Q its determinant. Fibonacci is the case P = 1, Q = −1, which is exactly the law (a + b − ab)/(1 + ab) adding 1 to itself.

One level up, the elliptic laws count the same way with elliptic divisibility (Somos) sequences, and the Lucas count is exactly their pinched edge, where the gluing number goes to zero. That ties this counting engine to the elliptic and soliton pages. It is known mathematics (Ward 1948), and it holds in every check.

The golden ratio is not a force running through the results. It shows up for one arithmetic reason, the number 5, and in the numbers our pages actually produced it turns up no more often than chance. The constants that do repeat above chance are e and its family, which is the ruler that turns every law into plain multiplication.

Verdict on each pattern, fundamental first

  • Keep as the lens
    One step, three kinds. Each law is a 2×2 step. Its three countries are the three kinds of 2×2 step: two walls (it stretches toward a wall), the border (it slides), no wall (it turns). This is the classification of Möbius maps, known since the 1800s. It is the shape underneath the 1-D laws, not a new prediction.
  • Keep as a tool
    Lucas sequences count every law. For any law and any value a, the n-fold sum is a·U(n)/(U(n) − Q·U(n−1)) with P = 2 + k·a and Q = 1 + k·a + d·a². Associativity becomes Lucas's addition rule, Cassini's identity becomes the determinant, and a wall is exactly where the determinant is zero. Known (Lucas 1878); new only as a placement of every law on one recurrence. Proved in Lean.
  • Not a force
    The golden ratio comes from 5, twice. The smallest irrational walls a whole-number law can have are the golden walls (discriminant 5, at k = ±1, d = −1): that is the resistor ladder and the Fibonacci law. And the first cycle a whole-number turn can never make is the 5-cycle, so a 5-cycle forces a golden trace: that is where Lee–Yang's 1.618 came from. Both facts are classical number theory. In the census φ appears at chance level, and every hit was put in by hand.
  • Explained
    e and its family repeat above chance. e, e − 1 and 1/e turn up on 5 to 6 independent pages, far above chance. That is the exponential ruler: the lens that turns the circle operation into multiplication, and rung flow. It confirms that the ruler is the shared object across pages.
  • Not one force
    Two different numbers once shared the letter q. Before 1.0 the pages used q for the map's second coordinate (now d, the curvature: relativity, twisted boosts) and for the gluing/colour number k + 1 (elliptic, solitons, q-counting, Lee–Yang). Parallels hold within each meaning, not across them. How the two meet is the subject of the map-of-directions work, so it is left there.
  • Holds, known
    One level up: elliptic laws count with Somos sequences. On an elliptic law the n-fold sum is read off an elliptic divisibility sequence W(n) (Ward 1948). On every landscape law that sequence is the Lucas sequence times a gauge, and the Lucas recurrence is the elliptic one with an extra factor Q^(n−1), proved in Lean. On the theta side W(n) = θ(n·z), and at gluing q = 0 the theta becomes the soliton's sinh, which is Lucas again. Worth keeping as the one counting engine across the landscape, elliptic and soliton pages; not new mathematics.
  • Not a force
    The golden ratio returns at the elliptic level, from 5 again. Curves can do what lines cannot: a rational point of order 5 (Mazur's list allows 1 to 10 and 12). The family of curves with such a point pinches back to a line only at b = φ⁵ and b = −φ⁻⁵, and there the point becomes the golden 5-cycle with turn number φ² or 1/φ². Its parameter is minus the fifth power of the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction, which tends to 1/φ as the gluing tightens. So φ is where 5-fold symmetry meets a line: the same 5 as before, seen from above. Classical (Klein, Ramanujan).

Where the golden ratio actually lives

Violet: the Fibonacci law adds 1 to itself and gets F(n)/F(n+1), marching into its wall 1/φ. Orange: its mirror image, the resistor ladder of the simple-laws page, marches into φ. Both have discriminant 5.

A turning step has period n exactly when its turn number P²/Q equals 4cos²(πm/n), the Beraha numbers. Filled dots are whole numbers (periods 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, the only cycles a whole-number law can make). Period 5 is the first gap, and its turn numbers are φ² and 1/φ².

One level up: elliptic counting

A landscape law adds by a 2×2 step and counts with a Lucas sequence. An elliptic law adds by the chord rule on a cubic curve and counts with an elliptic divisibility sequence: the n-fold sum of a point P has x(nP) = x(P) − W(n−1)·W(n+1)/W(n)², and W obeys W(m+n)W(m−n) = W(m+1)W(m−1)W(n)² − W(n+1)W(n−1)W(m)². On y² + y = x³ − x the sequence is 1, 1, -1, 1, 2, -1, -3, -5, 7, -4, -23, 29, 59 …, whole numbers with gcd(W m, W n) = |W gcd(m, n)|, just like Fibonacci.

Size of W(n) = θ(n·z), on a log scale, n = 1 to 24 (z fixed off the real line). At q = 0 the theta is the soliton's sinh, a Lucas sequence, and the dots climb in a straight line. Any gluing bends the line into a parabola: elliptic counting grows like n², line counting like n.

The curves with a point of order 5 form one family, y² + (1 − b)xy − by = x³ − bx², with discriminant b⁵(b² − 11b − 1). Glued with gluing q⁵, the family sits at b = −R(q)⁵, where R is the Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction (violet). As q → 1, R → 1/φ and b → −φ⁻⁵, the golden pinch (gold).

Constant census against a fair null

Every number printed to at least 4 significant digits in every page's check output (7,413 numbers, 33 pages) was matched against 21 named constants, also allowing the negative, the reciprocal and ±1 (the circle operation shifts by 1). The null keeps each number's first two digits and size and scrambles the rest, 1,000 times. A constant counts as a real repeat only if it beats its null.
