Laws and their shapes

Do shapes depend on shapes?

Inside the proofs, yes: shapes stack in a clear order, stick, then square, then box, then cube of any size. Across whole science laws, no: a law's shape says nothing about which laws it rests on. The one exact order is among the composition shapes, and that is the part worth following.

1. Proofs: bodies stand on smaller bodies

I read which theorems each proof actually uses, straight from Lean's compiled proof terms, for all 3,003 theorems in the library. Then I tagged each theorem with its gallery shape and counted uses that cross from one body to another.

The best order puts stick first, then square, then box and hyperbox (nearly tied), then cube of any size. Only 171 of 637 uses go against it. Shuffling the shape labels inside each Lean file gives 44% backwards on average, and none of 300 shuffles got as low as 27%. The point body has too few uses to place.

The lead moves line up the same way. Facts you read off sit at the bottom; dial and squash sit near the top.

Honest caveat: a lot of this is expected. Big statements get proved from small lemmas, so the order mostly describes how the library was built, bottom up. It is a fact about proofs, not about nature.

2. Families: tangled up close, clean from a distance

With all 58 shape families, the graph is full of cycles, mostly families with the same body using each other both ways. Keep only links with 12 or more uses and the cycles vanish: 20 links, one clean order.

3. The main exception: any size down to a stick

The biggest backwards flow is 43 uses from cube of any size to stick. Each one proves a law for every n, then sets n to one value. That is specialising, not building, so it bends the picture without breaking it.

4. Whole science laws: no order

I took 39 laws across physics, chemistry and biology, gave each the body that most of its proofs have, and drew 57 links between them. Each link is tagged: textbook derivation, my own reading (thermodynamics to information, general relativity to the dark sector, electromagnetism to charge conservation), or a use the Lean files show directly (special relativity to Thomas rotation, quantum noise to entropy, information to entropy).

Along those links, 13 go to a bigger body and 12 to a smaller one. That is what chance gives. Most laws are square shaped, and only by weak majorities, so the label is a loose description of the law.

A close look at one famous chain. Electromagnetism leads to special relativity, which leads to general relativity and then to Gravity Probe B. The shapes go hyperbox, square, square, point: down as often as up. The library's own files even run the other way from history, since the electromagnetism file imports the relativity files.

5. Composition shapes: one exact line

The landscape page sorts every two-number composition law by one discriminant into a few global shapes: two walls (the circle operation, called mult-add before 1.0), a border (addition), no wall (the turning law), and the torus above them. These do depend on each other exactly, and I proved each link in Lean.

  • Addition is the root: the circle operation is addition plus the corner ab.
  • Turning: the no-wall law is the two-wall law turned by i, and the turn keeps the border fixed.
  • Walls merging: bring the two walls together and you get the border, which is addition.
  • Torus pinched: pinch it once and you get the circle operation; pinch it twice and you get addition.
  • Zoom: rescaling never changes the shape.

This part is known math (formal group laws; singular cubic curves, as in Silverman's book, chapter III.2). What is new is that it is now checked in the library's own terms, in lean/ShapeDependencies.lean.

What's known and what's mine

  • Known: the composition chain, the textbook links between laws, and the fact that proofs build big from small.
  • Measured here: the size of the body order in this library (27% against 44%), the clean family order at 12 or more uses, and the chance level result for whole laws.
  • My reading: three of the 57 law links, and the lead below.

Is there a lead?

The proof shapes are not a lead about laws. Their order comes from how proofs get written, and it disappears at the level of whole laws.

The composition shapes are a modest lead. They are the only grouping here with an exact order, so the natural test is to sort physical laws by composition shape and see whether their dependencies follow addition, then walls, then turned or torus. A first guess, not yet tested: Galilean velocities (border) lead to special relativity (two walls), which leads to Thomas rotation (no wall); Shannon entropy (border) leads to Tsallis entropy (two walls). Much of this will likely reproduce known group contractions, so the real test is whether anything turns up that the contraction story does not already predict.
