Every proof in the library, drawn as a shape

Each of the library's Lean proofs is drawn from its own source as a mult-add shape, then grouped by that shape alone. Fields never enter the grouping, so proofs from far-apart subjects land in the same drawer when their geometry matches.

The rung sheet: at each height s, the curve of all a, b with a combined with b at rung s equal to 1. The outer corner of the square is at the bottom, the line (addition, s = 0) and the hyperbola (mult-add, s = 1) in the middle, the axes at the top. Each numbered pin is a shape family, placed at the highest rung its statements use.

    Same shape, different worlds

    These proofs have exactly the same shape (same size, same frame, same moves) and come from different broad fields. Each pair or trio is the strongest match the rules found; ambiguous shapes were left out.

    The gallery

    One tile per proof. Proofs with the same tile have the same shape. A dashed tile is a shape the rules could not read with confidence; open it to see why.

    Reading a glyph

    The body is the statement's size: how many rods are multiplied together in its biggest piece. The overlay is the proof's leading move. Small marks along the bottom are the other moves; a mark at the top right is the frame, how the two sides meet.

    How shapes were read

    Nothing was assigned by hand. Scripts read the Lean files, and a Lean program reads the compiled library (the library itself is untouched); the same rules apply to every proof:

    PartRead fromRule
    Size (body)The statementIts polynomial degree in its variables, with the library's definitions expanded (mult-add a b counts as a + b + ab). Degree 0 is a point, 1 a rod, 2 a rectangle, 3 a box, 4 to 9 a hyperbox. A power with a symbolic exponent, a fold over a list or a recursive definition run n times is a cube of any size.
    FrameThe statementEqual: the same picture. ≤ or <: one fits inside the other. ≠ or ¬: they cannot match. Divides or congruent: it tiles evenly. ∃: a piece is found that fits. ↔: each holds the other.
    MovesThe proof, the definitions it unfolds and the lemmas it citesThe proof engine's moves, matched by a fixed table of lemma names and patterns: lift, mult-add, swap, regroup, collapse, squash, dial, flatten, extrude. Extra marks: explode (corner pieces), repeat (induction), measure (rods compared by length), tally (checked by counting). Swap, regroup and collapse are also read from the two sides of the statement directly.
    Compiled movesThe proof term Lean compiled (engine/Extract.lean)Every constant the kernel-checked proof uses, with Lean's own helper lemmas opened and the statement's own words removed, matched by a second table. Grind's ring normaliser counts as explode (a polynomial as a sum of monomials is the box broken into corner pieces); omega and linear arithmetic count as measure; decide counts as tally. Used for the leading move only when the proof's own text names none.
    Size checkThe engine's Reader, run on the elaborated statementThe proof engine reads each statement as a picture (lifted products, mult-adds, dialed corners, mult-add powers, squashes). Its largest box sets the size when the polynomial reading fails.
    FamilySize and movesSize plus the leading move, the first present in this order: squash, dial, flatten, regroup, swap, explode, collapse, repeat; otherwise plain. Families under four proofs are pooled by move, and the last few into odd ones out.
    Height on the sheetThe statements = 0 for rods (addition), 1 for rectangles, boxes and hyperboxes (mult-add), 2 for any-size cubes (the fused exponent), 3 and 4 for tetration and pentation; flattening proofs drop a rung. A family sits at its median.

    Scripts and data: math-theory/proof-gallery/ in the project folder (parse.py, degree.py, features.py, cluster.py, build_data.py; gallery.json holds every proof's shape), with the Lean extract from math-theory/proof-engine/lean/Extract.lean. Sheet curves from the rung-shape work.
