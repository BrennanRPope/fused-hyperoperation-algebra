Adding two tangles is the circle operation, and the weight is the value of a loop

Cut a knot diagram open and you have a tangle: a box with four loose ends. Kauffman's bracket writes any tangle as a mix of two plain ones, f parts "two arcs across" and g parts "two arcs down". Put two tangles side by side and the mix follows the dial a + b + k·ab exactly, with k = d, the value of one closed loop. The corner term is a loop trapped in the middle. Closing the tangle is the lift.

Each tangle is either across ([0], weight f) or down ([∞], weight g). Side by side there are four corners, as in the circle-operation square. Three give a plain tangle back. The fourth, down beside down, traps the red loop, and a loop is worth d = −A² − A⁻².

The sum, read off the four corners:

(f₁, g₁) + (f₂, g₂) = (f₁f₂,  f₁g₂ + g₁f₂ + d·g₁g₂)

Call x = g/f the tangle's share of "down". Then

x(S + T) = x(S) + x(T) + d·x(S)·x(T)

That is the circle operation ∘ₖ (called mult-add before 1.0) with dial k = d. Its lift is 1 + d·x = ⟨D(T)⟩/f, where D closes the tangle by joining the two left ends and the two right ends. Closing a sum gives a connected sum, and brackets multiply:

⟨D(S + T)⟩ = ⟨D(S)⟩ · ⟨D(T)⟩

Checked against the full state sum over the cube for 40 random tangles up to 8 crossings, both closures. Proved for any ring in Lean (ratio_dial, closeD_tsum).

A twist is one step of the q-dial, and the twists walk round a circle

Rescale the share to y = A²·g/f. Adding one crossing sends y to q·y + 1, with q = −A⁻⁴. In Jones's variable t = A⁻⁴ that is q = −t. So n crossings in a row give y = [n]_q = 1 + q + … + q^(n−1): n copies of 1 under the dial ∘₁ with k = q − 1, the same q-integers as the q page.

The step y ↦ qy + 1 has two walls (fixed points): ∞ and c = 1/(1 − q). It turns the plane about c by q. When q sits on the unit circle the twists are the corners of a regular polygon with side 1, starting at 0. At q = e^(2πi/m) the polygon closes after m twists.

Slide q's angle to zero. The circle grows, the wall c runs off to infinity and meets the other wall, and the polygon opens into the integers 0, 1, 2, 3. That is the point A⁴ = −1, where the loop value d is 0 and the dial becomes plain addition. There y is Conway's fraction: a twist adds 1 to it, and two tangles side by side add their fractions.

At q = 1 the two dials are continued fractions, and the numerator is the determinant

Stacking a crossing under a tangle is the same step turned a quarter: 1/y ↦ q⁻¹·(1/y) + 1. At q = 1 the side step adds 1 to the fraction F and the stacked step adds 1 to 1/F. Alternate them a₁, a₂, a₃ times and F is a continued fraction. The two steps are the matrices [[1,1],[0,1]] and [[1,0],[1,1]], which generate SL(2, ℤ), so every rational tangle is a walk in the Möbius group.

Close the tangle on top and you get a knot whose determinant, |V(−1)|, is the numerator of F. At general q the same two steps give the q-deformed rationals of Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko: the figure-eight tangle's y is exactly their [5/2]_q = (1 + 2q + q² + q³)/(1 + q).

The landscape's bend does not appear. Every law here sits on the dial line (bend 0). The stacked law is the dial seen through y ↦ 1/y, which moves its identity to ∞, so it is not a landscape point. The Möbius structure is real, but it comes from two dials, not from a bend.

The loop value picks out the Jones polynomial's special points

The loop value d also sets a Potts model: d² = 2 + t + 1/t is the number of colours Q (Jones 1989; the bracket is a Tutte polynomial, Thistlethwaite 1987). The classical evaluations sit exactly where Q is a whole number:

  • Q = 0, t = −1, d = 0: the determinant, Conway's fraction, the counting point q = 1.
  • Q = 1, t = e^(2πi/3): V = 1 for every knot.
  • Q = 2, t = i: V = ±1, the Arf invariant (Murakami 1986).
  • Q = 3, t = e^(iπ/3): |V| is a power of √3 (Lickorish and Millett 1986).
  • Q = 4, t = 1: V = 1 for every knot.

This is the same list the Lee–Yang page met as Beraha's numbers. One correction to the q page: there, factoring d put the counting point at t = 1. The twist step pins q = −t, which puts the counting point at t = −1, where the determinant lives.

Torus knots carry a ratio of q-numbers

The Alexander polynomial of the torus knot T(p, q) is (t^pq − 1)(t − 1)/((t^p − 1)(t^q − 1)). Read with q-numbers it is [q]_(t^p) / [q]_t, a ratio of two dial powers. Its zeros are the pq-th roots of unity that are neither p-th nor q-th roots: (p − 1)(q − 1) dots on the circle. For T(2, n) it is [n]_(−t) = 1 − t + t² − … + t^(n−1): the twist's own q-integer, at the same q = −t.

Connected sum multiplies Jones, Alexander and HOMFLY. So the "circle-operation value" P − 1 of a knot turns connected sum into ∘₁ exactly. That is honest relabelling: it is the lift read backwards, and it predicts nothing new.

Lead worth following?

Not as new knot theory. Every formula here was known, and the q-deformation of Conway's fraction is already mapped by Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko. What the circle-operation reading adds is a clean geometric reason for each piece: the corner is a trapped loop, closing is the lift, and Conway's fraction is the dial at weight zero, where the twist's two walls merge.

It is worth keeping as a lens and as a teaching picture. One small test is left: tangles with four ends only ever reach the dial line. Tangles with six ends have a five-dimensional bracket space, and that is the first place a bend could appear. That is a question, not yet a lead.
