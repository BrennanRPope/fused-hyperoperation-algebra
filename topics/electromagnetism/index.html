Electromagnetism through the circle-operation lens

Electromagnetism fits under the same lens as gravity, in three exact places, and it comes first in the story: Maxwell's waves are what set the dial that special relativity and then gravity run on. Nothing here is new physics; it is known physics seen through one shared lens, with the renamings marked as renamings.

The answer, judged on its own merits

  • Same structure: electromagnetism sets the dial. The wave equation that comes out of Maxwell's equations survives a dial boost only when q = −1/c², and c² = 1/(ε₀μ₀) (CODATA: c²ε₀μ₀ = 1.000000000000). A Galilean boost (q = 0) breaks it. This is the historical reason relativity grew out of electromagnetism.
  • Same structure: the field rides the boost. Split E and B along the two light lines and a boost multiplies one part by the Doppler factor D and the other by 1/D. Their ratio is multiplied by D² = 1 + d(v), the circle operation's lift, and two boosts multiply the D's, so the d's the circle operation. A light wave sits on one light line (the wall) and its energy density falls by exactly that lift.
  • Same structure: loop turn = curvature × area. The phase a charge picks up around a loop is charge × enclosed flux, the same rule as gravity's "a loop returns boosted by curvature × area". On a lattice each plaquette is the circle operation of its four links, and neighbouring plaquettes combine by the circle operation into the bigger loop (discrete Stokes).
  • Renamings, said plainly. Writing a phase as a = e^(iθ) − 1 turns multiplication into the circle operation, but that works for any group of numbers under multiplication, so it adds nothing by itself. Calling F "the ab corner" of ∇A is notation; the content underneath is Clifford's split of a product into a dot part and a wedge part, and ∇F = J is Hestenes's 1966 spacetime algebra.
  • A small frame of our own: the gauge phase and the boost are the same rule, a ✳ ā = 0, at mirror dials: q = +1 (a circle that wraps and never meets the wall) and q = −1 (a hyperbola that runs up to the light walls). Exact and tidy; a way of seeing, not a prediction.
  • Why electromagnetism is linear and gravity isn't: phases commute, so the plain circle operation (which is commutative) is exactly electromagnetism's group law, a constant potential makes no field, and the field carries no charge. Boosts and Yang–Mills phases don't commute; the gap between the two orders is the commutator, which is the field's self-coupling. Known in substance (abelian versus non-abelian gauge theory); the circle-operation reading is ours.
  • Where it stops. The circle operation does not give the field equation itself; the source law ∇F = J comes from outside, as Einstein's equations did on the gravity page. Fields in empty space add by plain addition (the k = 0 rung), not the circle operation. Born–Infeld's field cap has the exact shape of the light-speed wall but does not compose by Einstein's rule.
  • No new prediction and no lead toward new physics here. What is worth keeping is the bridge: electromagnetism is the commuting half of the same loop rule gravity uses, which is why the river picture's swirl turning out to be teleparallel torsion (a gauge field) fits the family.

1. Electromagnetism sets the dial

Take a boost with dial q: x′ = γ(x − vt), t′ = γ(t + q v x). Put it into the wave operator ∂²/∂t² − c²∂²/∂x² that Maxwell's equations give for light. A cross term ∂²/∂x′∂t′ appears with coefficient −2γ²v(1 + c²q). It vanishes only at q = −1/c², and then the operator comes back with the same wave speed. At q = 0 (Galileo) the cross term is −2γ²v and the wave equation changes form.

Maxwell's constants fix c: c² ε₀ μ₀ = 1, so in SI units the dial is q = −ε₀μ₀ = −1.1127 × 10⁻¹⁷ s²/m². From there the relativity page takes over: velocities compose by (u + v)/(1 − q u v), and d(u) = 2u/(1 − u) composes by the circle operation.

Known: Lorentz 1904, Einstein 1905. The dial reading is the project's landscape law (relativity page). Checks A1 to A3; Lean wave_cross_zero_iff, galilean_cross, wave_speed_kept.

2. Boosting E and B: the field on the light cone

Write the boost with the Doppler factor D instead of v. Then γ = (D² + 1)/2D and γv = (D² − 1)/2D, everything is rational, and the textbook boost of the fields along x splits cleanly:

  • E_y + B_z and E_z − B_y are multiplied by 1/D; E_y − B_z and E_z + B_y by D.
  • So the ratio of the two parts is multiplied by D² = (1 + v)/(1 − v) = 1 + d(v): the circle operation's lift. Their product does not change (a squeeze, the same as T² − X² for clocks and rulers).
  • Two boosts make one with D = D₁D₂. The speed of D₁D₂ is Einstein's sum, and D² − 1 composes by the circle operation: (D₁D₂)² − 1 = (D₁² − 1) ✳ (D₂² − 1).
  • E² − B² and E·B are the same in every frame. A light wave has both equal to 0 (a null field): it sits on a light line, the wall. Boosted along its direction its amplitude is divided by D and its energy density by D², the inverse lift.
  • A pure electric field, boosted, gains B_z = −γv E_y: magnetism is electricity seen from a moving frame (Purcell's picture).

The same boost is the Clifford sandwich F′ = R F R̃ with rotor R = cosh(φ/2) − sinh(φ/2) σₓ, where D = e^φ: the half-velocities of the Clifford page, now acting on the field.

Known: the transformation of fields (Lorentz, Einstein 1905), light-cone components (standard in radiation theory), rotor form (Hestenes 1966). Checks B0 to B7; Lean lc_plus, lc_minus, lc_pair2, lc_ratio, doppler_circ, dop_compose, vD_mul, inv1_boost, inv2_boost, null_stays_null, wave_boost, purcell.

3. The gauge phase as a dial

A charged particle's wavefunction carries a phase e^(iθ), and electromagnetism is the freedom to change that phase from place to place. Write the phase as 1 + a. Then:

  • Phases compose by the circle operation: (e^(iθ₁) − 1) ✳ (e^(iθ₂) − 1) = e^(i(θ₁+θ₂)) − 1. This is a renaming of multiplication.
  • A number is a unit phase exactly when a ✳ ā = 0: its complex conjugate is its circle inverse, because a ✳ ā = |1 + a|² − 1. Unit phases are closed under ✳, and the wall a = −1 is never one.
  • A boost, written as a split-complex number 1 + a = cosh φ + j sinh φ with j² = +1, obeys the very same rule a ✳ ā = 0.
  • In half-angle charts the two compose by the landscape law at mirror dials: tan(θ/2) at q = +1, (t₁ + t₂)/(1 − t₁t₂); tanh(φ/2) at q = −1, (s₁ + s₂)/(1 + s₁s₂).

Gauge invariance itself checks out exactly: shifting the potential by a gradient, A → A + ∇χ and φ → φ − ∂χ/∂t, leaves E and B unchanged.

The circle-and-hyperbola pairing is the project's landscape picture (q page, Clifford page item 02); the a ✳ ā = 0 statement for both is this page's. Checks C1 to C5; Lean maC_conj, maC_lift, phase_closed, wall_not_phase, maS_conj, boost_closed, half_charts.

4. Loops and lattices: loop turn = curvature × area

Carry a charge around a closed loop and its phase turns by charge × (magnetic flux through the loop). With a uniform field this is exact for any polygon: the loop integral of A equals B × area (20 random polygons, exact rationals). Around a thin solenoid the field on the path is zero, yet every loop that goes round it picks up the full flux and every loop that misses it picks up nothing (Aharonov and Bohm 1959; seen by Tonomura and others in 1986).

On a lattice (Wilson 1974) each link carries a phase 1 + a. A plaquette's value is the circle operation of its four links, the two backward links entering as circle inverses −a/(1 + a). For a potential that varies linearly it equals e^(iε²F) − 1 exactly. Two plaquettes side by side the circle operation into the big loop because the shared link runs both ways and cancels: Stokes's theorem in circle-operation form.

This is the place where electromagnetism and gravity share the most: both are connections, and in both the turn around a small loop is the curvature times the area. The difference is what turns. In electromagnetism it is a phase, and phases commute.

Known: Weyl 1929 (gauge phase), Yang–Mills 1954, Aharonov–Bohm 1959, Wilson 1974, Wu–Yang 1975 (loop phases as the full content of a gauge field). The circle-operation form of plaquettes and Stokes is this page's rewriting. Checks C6 to C8; Lean plaquette, stokes, stokes_circ.

5. F as the corner of a Clifford product

The Clifford thread found that with multivector inputs the circle operation stays associative and the gap between the two orders is exactly the wedge: x ✳ y − y ✳ x = 2 x∧y. In spacetime algebra a vector product splits the same way, ab = a·b + a∧b, so the corner of the lift square (1 + a)(1 + b) is a dot part plus a wedge part.

Put the derivative ∇ in for a and the potential A in for b. The corner ∇A splits into ∇·A, which is a gauge choice (the Lorenz gauge sets it to 0), and ∇∧A, which is exactly the field F, with E = −∇φ − ∂A/∂t and B = ∇×A. The field strength is the wedge, the order-gap part of the corner.

Honest status: this is notation that lines up. ∇ is an operator, not a number, so the "lift" (1 + ∇)(1 + A) has no meaning of its own; the content is the dot-plus-wedge split, which is Clifford's.

Checks D1, D2; Clifford page item 08 (Lean G4.vector_comm_wedge).

6. Maxwell as one equation

In spacetime algebra F = E + IB is one object, and Maxwell's four equations are one: ∇F = J, with J = ρ plus the current. Its vector part is Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law; its trivector part is "no magnetic charge" and Faraday's law. Checked symbolically with general E(t, x, y, z) and B(t, x, y, z).

  • Charge conservation is automatic. ∇∇ is the wave operator, a scalar, so ∇(∇F) is again a field-type object with no scalar part; hence ∇·J = 0 for every F. In momentum space: k_μ k_ν F^μν = 0 for any antisymmetric F (Lean charge_conserved).
  • The same geometry gives gravity's ∇·G = 0 (the Bianchi identity, checked on every river on the GR page), which forces conservation of energy.
  • FF = (E² − B²) + 2(E·B) I: the two invariants are the scalar and the pseudoscalar parts of the field's square, and light is the case FF = 0.
  • In the Lorenz gauge, ∇∇A = J is the wave equation, and ∇∧(∇∧A) = 0 always (no magnetic charge is an identity, not a law).

Known: Hestenes 1966 (Space-Time Algebra); Doran and Lasenby 2003. Checks D3 to D8, F1.

7. Where the lens stops

  • The source law comes from outside. The circle operation explains how fields transform and how phases compose, not why the field obeys ∇F = J (or, as a Lagrangian, why the action is F²). That is the same gap the gravity page found for Einstein's equations.
  • Superposition is plain addition. Maxwell in empty space is linear: two solutions add by ordinary addition, the k = 0 rung, with no corner term. The circle operation enters through the phases and the boosts, not through how fields pile up.
  • Born–Infeld shares the wall's shape, not the law. Born and Infeld (1934) capped the field the way relativity caps speed: E = D/√(1 + D²/b²), the same curve as v = p/√(1 + p²). But Born–Infeld fields add by adding D (Gauss's law), and E(D₁ + D₂) ≠ E₁ ⊕ E₂ (0.9029 against 0.9479 in the example). ATLAS light-by-light data rule out a Born–Infeld scale below about 100 GeV (Ellis, Mavromatos and You 2017).
  • Real electrodynamics turns nonlinear only near the Schwinger field m²c³/(eħ) = 1.32 × 10¹⁸ V/m, through quantum effects, not through a circle-operation rule.

Checks E1 to E3; Lean bi_cap, bi_den_pos, bi_inverse. Source for the bound: arXiv:1703.08450.

8. On to gravity

Einstein built on electromagnetism, and the lens shows the steps. Electromagnetism hands special relativity its dial, and special relativity hands gravity the twist rule. Gravity then keeps electromagnetism's shape and changes one thing:
