Each step of a fluid is a circle-operation cube, and vortex stretching lives in its far corner

Give every fluid particle its jet, and one step x ↦ x + h·u(x) changes volume by the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0) of its three stretch rates. The cube's layers are the three invariants turbulence research already plots. All of it is known mathematics; the circle-operation view adds one picture that holds it together, and 46 new Lean theorems.

1. A jet is a truncated product

The k-jet of a function at x, its value and first k derivatives, is f(x + ε) in the ring R[ε]/(εk+1). Multiplying jets is multiplying functions, which is Leibniz's rule. Write a jet as value × (1 + ν). Then the jet of a product combines the ν's by the circle operation: (1 + ν)(1 + μ) = 1 + (ν ∘₁ μ).

  • Jet order is cube depth. In the k-jet every ν is a multiple of ε, so a corner of j factors sits at εj. Layers above k vanish. 1-jets keep layer 1 only (relative slopes just add); 2-jets keep the pair corners and lose the triple corner.
  • No wall in jet space. Because ν is nilpotent, the way back is a finite sum, 1 − ν + ν², and 1 + ν is never zero.
  • Composition is the circle operation only to first order. The chain rule multiplies slopes, so f′ − 1 combines by the circle operation; the higher terms (Faà di Bruno) are not the circle operation.
%%SECANT%%

The corner weight is a gap. In R[ε]/(ε² − kε), (1 + aε)(1 + bε) = 1 + (a ∘k b)ε, and the element is exactly the pair of values at x and at x + k. Shrinking k merges the two points into a tangent: the dual numbers, ε² = 0, the corner dropped. The wall 1 + ka = 0 is just a zero at the second point.

Known: jets (Ehresmann 1951), Weil algebras (Weil 1953), x + y + xy is the multiplicative formal group law, and R[ε]/(ε² − kε) is the ring of forward differences.

2. Navier–Stokes is a surface in jet space

A field of jets, every particle carrying its derivatives, is a section of the jet bundle. Navier–Stokes is a surface in the space of 2-jets, and a solution is a field whose 2-jet never leaves it:

ui,t + uj ui,j + p,i − ν ui,jj = 0,    uj,j = 0

The term ujui,j is the only place where two jet coordinates multiply. Read with a dual number it is the ε-part of u(x + εu): the field read where its own first-order step carries it. So the self-feeding term is a self-composition, and Navier–Stokes needs only its first order, which is exactly where composition is the circle operation. This is the Cartan picture of a PDE as a submanifold of jet space, developed by the Vinogradov school.

3. What the view measures: the cube of one step

The step x ↦ x + h·u(x) has gradient I + h∇u. Its volume change is the drawing at the top:

det(I + h∇u) = 1 + h·P + h²·Q + h³·R,    and in principal axes   = 1 + (ha ∘₁ hb ∘₁ hc)
  • P, the slabs, is the divergence. Incompressible flow sets it to 0.
  • Q, the rods, is ¼|ω|² − ½|S|²: rotation against strain.
  • R, the far corner, is det S + ¼ ω·Sω: the strain amplifying itself plus vortex stretching.

On a periodic box the step map wraps the box onto itself exactly once, so its average volume change is zero for every h. Every layer must therefore average to zero. Layer 2 gives ⟨|ω|²⟩ = 2⟨|S|²⟩. Layer 3 gives Betchov's relation ⟨ω·Sω⟩ = −4⟨det S⟩. Real turbulence stretches vortices on average, so the strain's far corner is negative: two directions stretching, one squeezing. These layers are null Lagrangians; on a grid the 2D one is an exact divergence only when the corner is kept, which the Lean file proves.

4. The far corner, the Osgood line and the one-rung gap

Drop viscosity and the nonlocal part of the pressure and the gradient feeds on itself (restricted Euler). The layers then drive each other: Q′ = 3R and R′ = −⅔Q² (with R = det ∇u; Cantwell writes R with the opposite sign), and 27R² + 4Q³, minus the discriminant of the cube polynomial, stays fixed. On its zero set, the Vieillefosse tail, |R|′ grows like |R|4/3. The Osgood integral ∫dR/R4/3 is finite, so it blows up in finite time (about 1.26 time units from |R| = 1, checked numerically). In 2D the same flow is frozen: a square has no third layer, and −2A² + tr(A²)·I = 0 exactly.

Lead, and what is not yet shown. The dimension page measured the 3D gap as what dissipation lacks: one rung. Here the source is visible: the far corner supplies power-law self-feeding, exponent 4/3, on the wrong side of the Osgood line y·log y, and 2D has none. Viscosity and the nonlocal pressure must pull it back. Whether those two measures of the gap are one quantity has not been shown; that would be real work, not a re-description.
