Only two notches on the dial keep true and false: OR and XOR

Put 0 for false and 1 for true. The circle operation a + b + k·ab gives 0, 1, 1 on three corners of the truth table for every k. The fourth corner, 1 with 1, lands at 2 + k. It stays a truth value at exactly two settings: k = −1, which is OR, and k = −2, which is XOR. Turn the knob and watch the corner.

AND is the corner cell itself, the product ab. It is the same at every k. So the dial holds the whole of Boolean logic: OR and AND make the Boolean lattice, XOR and AND make the Boolean ring.

The leak is one parabola, and it works in any ring

This is not special to 0 and 1. Take any a and b that equal their own squares (idempotents: on sets they are indicator functions, in a matrix ring they are projections). Then the dial gives back an idempotent up to one leak term:

(a ✳ₖ b)² − (a ✳ₖ b) = (k + 1)(k + 2)·ab

The two roots are OR and XOR. Read with q = k + 1, the roots are q = 0 and q = −1, and n trues folded together give the q-number [n]_q: always 1 for OR, and 1, 0, 1, 0 (parity) for XOR. Every other q leaves 0 and 1 already at n = 2.

The other Boolean laws leak the same way. Distributing AND over the dial misses by k·abc(1 − a); absorption a ∨ (a ∧ b) misses by ab(1 − a). Both vanish exactly when a² = a. So Boolean algebra is the dial with its inputs pinned to 0 and 1, and nothing more.

Each notch has a wall, and the wall is a truth value

Lines b ↦ m ✳ₖ b for m = 0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1. Left, OR: every line passes through (1, 1). Right, XOR: every line pivots on (½, ½).

The dial's wall is −1/k, the value that swallows everything. For OR it is 1: true OR anything is true. For XOR it is ½: a fair coin XOR any bit is a fair coin. That is Shannon's one-time pad (1949). Encrypt a message bit with a fair key bit and the ciphertext is exactly ½, whatever the message, and independent of it.

Fuzzy logic: Hamacher's family is the dial, read in odds

Fuzzy logic lets truth run from 0 to 1 and asks for an "or" that is associative, monotone and has 0 as identity (a t-conorm). Hamacher (1978) found every such rule that is a ratio of polynomials. His family is

S_γ(a, b) = (a + b + (γ − 2)ab) / (1 + (γ − 1)ab)

On the landscape that is the point p = γ − 2, q = 1 − γ, so the whole family is the line p + q = −1. The statistics page found this same line as the unions with certainty as a wall. Measure truth by its odds, o = x/(1 − x), and the bend disappears:

odds(S_γ(a, b)) = odds(a) ✳γ odds(b)

Hamacher's parameter is the dial, read on odds. γ = 0 adds odds, γ = 1 combines them by the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0) (the probabilistic sum a + b − ab), γ = 2 is the Einstein sum (a + b)/(1 + ab), relativity's point. The t-norms ("and") are the mirror images under x ↦ 1 − x, De Morgan's law, and are the dial on odds against.

Grey curve: the landscape's border, a double wall. Teal: the stretch γ from 0 to 2, where the rule is also a probability law (a copula). Amber, running on to the lower right: γ above 2, still a fuzzy "or" but no longer a union of events. At γ = 0 the line touches the border.

Sets have no wall, until you allow negative sets

For finite sets, |A ⊔ B ⊔ A×B| = a ✳ b, and adding a spare point to each side turns this into a plain product, minus the spare pair. Sizes are never negative, so finite sets have no wall and no inverses.

Count by Euler characteristic instead (Schanuel 1991): a point counts 1, an open interval counts −1, a product counts the product. Now the open interval is the wall. Glue the spare point to it and you get [0, 1), which counts 0, and 0 times anything is 0. Two open intervals count −2, the circle operation's other unit: X ⊔ X ⊔ X×X counts 0.

The logic of the rungs: arithmetic switches on at rung 2

The axioms page showed the circle operation alone is multiplication in disguise, a decidable theory (Skolem 1930, Mostowski 1952), and that the circle operation with the successor carries all of arithmetic. One more step finishes the ladder. The fused exponent x ⊛ a = (x + 1)^(a + 1) − 1 alone defines the rungs below it:

b ✳ c = d  ⇔  for every x, (x ⊛ b) ⊛ c = x ⊛ d
b + c + 1 = d  ⇔  for every x, (x ⊛ b) ✳ (x ⊛ c) = x ⊛ d

and 0 is its only right identity, so the successor is b + 0 + 1. With +, × and the successor definable, the first-order theory of rung 2 by itself is undecidable (Gödel, Church). Lean proves the three definitions.

Which single rungs, or pairs, carry full arithmetic. Teal: decidable (a machine can settle every statement). Red: undecidable.

Lead worth following?

A small one, in fuzzy logic. The odds reading turns a whole classical family into one dial line, and it predicts something checkable: any fuzzy rule built from the dial on some other coordinate (log-odds, survival, a power of x) is again associative, and the landscape tells you which coordinates land back on a rational rule. A short search for the coordinates that send the dial to rational t-conorms would either recover Hamacher's theorem in one line or find a gap in it. That is worth an afternoon.

The Boolean part is the known algebra of 0 and 1 seen through the lift. It is tidy, it explains why exactly two operations exist, and it closes the question; it does not open a new one.

The rung ladder is a clean statement, but it is a re-reading of a folklore fact (exponentiation defines multiplication), not a new direction.
