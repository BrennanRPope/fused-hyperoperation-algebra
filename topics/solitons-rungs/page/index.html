Mult-add is the q = 0 edge of a bigger shape, and solitons live on that edge

Your sense was right, in a precise and well-known way. The soliton cube is the q = 0 face of a lattice sum (the theta function of a Riemann surface). Turning q on glues each soliton into a repeating wave. One matrix holds both of your dials: q on its diagonal, the collision weight A off it, and both work the same way, as a weight paid once for every pair.

The soliton cube is one square of a whole lattice

Two solitons have the tau function 1 + w₁ + w₂ + A·w₁w₂, your weighted mult-add cube, with w = e^η the exponential of each wave's phase. The periodic waves of the same equation use a theta function, a sum over every lattice point (n₁, n₂):

Θ = Σ q₁^(n₁(n₁−1)/2) · q₂^(n₂(n₂−1)/2) · A^(n₁n₂) · w₁^n₁ · w₂^n₂

The q exponent n(n−1)/2 is zero only for n = 0 and n = 1. So at q = 0 every lattice point dies except the four corners of the unit square, and what is left is exactly the soliton cube. Turn q on and weight leaks onto the rest of the lattice.

The numbers in the exponent form one symmetric matrix, the period matrix of a Riemann surface. Its off-diagonal entries are log A, your mult-add weights, which say how solitons push each other. Its diagonal entries are log q, the gluing numbers, which say how each wave closes into a loop. Both are a weight paid once per pair: A for each pair of different solitons, q for each pair of copies of the same wave. Solitons are the corner of this space where every handle of the surface pinches shut, the same degeneration that turns the Tate curve into mult-add at q = 0 (already in the library as tate_q0_is_multAdd).

q turns one soliton into a train of copies

For a single wave, Jacobi's triple product (1829) says the theta function is a product over the q-orbit of w:

Θ(w; q) = (q; q)∞ · (1 + w) · Π (1 + w·qᵐ)(1 + qᵐ/w)

Each factor is a 1 + x lift, so Θ/(q; q)∞ − 1 is the mult-add of every point of the orbit (check 4b, error 10⁻⁴⁰). In wave terms, the periodic (cnoidal) KdV wave is an exact sum of identical copies of the q = 0 soliton, spaced −log q apart. Toda found this first and Boyd gave a second proof in 1984.

Geometrically, gluing is the annulus between radius |q| and radius 1 with its two rims sewn together, which makes a torus. Multiplying w by q is the same as mult-adding q − 1 to w − 1, and q − 1 = E(log q). So the gluing step itself is a rung-1 move, on the same rung as the soliton's own w − 1.

One whole rung step makes the soliton

A soliton is built in two moves. The phase η = kx − k³t moves by plain addition, which is rung 0. The exponential w = e^η = 1 + E(η) lifts it to rung 1, where solitons combine by the weighted mult-add cube. Then the solution reads it back down: u = 2·∂²log τ, and log τ is E⁻¹(τ − 1), exactly one step of the rung flow.

So I tested the rung flow at every step size t from 0 to 2.5: take E⁻ᵗ of the exponential and differentiate twice. Only t = 1 gives a KdV soliton. Below one step the right side keeps growing, so there is no lump. Above one step the tail dips negative and fades only slowly.

This matches Cole and Hopf's trick for Burgers' equation (1950–51), where one log and one derivative turn a linear heat flow into a shock. KdV uses the same one-step log with two derivatives.

Collisions take their own road from rung 0 to rung 1

For two solitons the cube is a + b + A·ab. To second order that is also what rung s looks like with s = A, so a pair of solitons seems to sit at a fractional rung. The real test comes with three bodies, by reading off the corner of the cube.

  • Solitons: each corner carries the product of its pair weights, so equal weights give A, A³, A⁶ for two, three and four solitons. KdV forces this: adding any three-body term to the corner breaks the equation (check 2b).
  • Rung s: the corners are s, s(3s−1)/2, s(2s−1)(3s−1)/2, from n!·[yⁿ]E^s(y).

They agree for two bodies. For three they agree only at s = 0, ½ and 1, and the meeting at ½ is gone by four bodies. So solitons don't sit on a fractional rung. They follow a second road, with addition at one end (A = 0, equal speeds merge) and mult-add at the other (A = 1, no interaction). Real KdV pairs always have 0 ≤ A < 1 (check 2d).

The soliton road has a name of its own: the series Σ A^(n(n−1)/2) yⁿ/n!. It uses the same triangular exponent as q in the theta function, which is why the collision dial and the gluing dial behave alike.

q is a second direction, not a rung height

q does not move a soliton up or down the rungs. Every wave in the q family is built at the same rung, from the same rung-1 cube. What q changes is how many copies are glued in and how tightly.

Its two ends still line up with rungs 0 and 1. At q = 0 you have a lone soliton, which carries every harmonic. As q goes to 1 the copies crowd together and the wave flattens into its mean plus a tiny cosine: a ripple height of 10⁻¹³ at q = 0.9. A ripple sits next to E's fixed point at 0, where a + b + ab is just a + b because ab is negligible, so the ripple's waves simply add, like linear waves. One number tracks the slide: the second harmonic over the first equals sech(2π² / (−log q)), which is 1 at q = 0 and falls to 0 as q goes to 1 (check 4f).

So q does give solitons a place, at the q = 0 end of a line that runs from rung-1 behaviour to rung-0 behaviour. It gets there by shrinking the wave toward the fixed point, not by passing through fractional rungs.

A soliton's path is the crease of a max

log τ is the log-sum-exp of the four corner heights 0, η₁, η₂ and η₁ + η₂ + log A, so it is never more than log 4 above their maximum (check 3a). Where the maximum switches from one corner to another, log τ bends, and that bend is the soliton. The space-time paths are the creases of a max, a piecewise straight graph. That picture is the tropical limit of Dimakis and Müller-Hoissen (2013). The small offset at the crossing is log A, and the fast soliton's measured jump matches it to four digits (check 3b).

Leads worth following

Worth following: draw the soliton road as a sheet

The soliton road F_A(y) = Σ A^(n(n−1)/2) yⁿ/n! runs between the same two rungs as the rung flow and splits from it at three bodies. Its log counts connected graphs, with your mult-add excess A − 1 as the weight on each edge. Why follow it: it is a second path from addition to mult-add that comes from physics rather than from iterating E, and it carries q's triangular exponent, so the gluing dial and the collision dial would share one picture. Next step: draw its level curves the way the rung sheet was drawn and lay the two sheets over each other.

Worth following: waves with both dials on

A soliton riding on a cnoidal background is a genus-2 theta solution with one q at 0 and the other above it, so A and q act at the same time. These solutions are known. Reading them on the rungs has not been tried. Why follow it: it is the one place where the claim "q and A are the same kind of weight" can fail, so it is a real test of the picture.

Not worth following: fractional rung steps on a soliton

Only t = 1 gives a soliton. The fractional profiles solve nothing and never form a lump.
