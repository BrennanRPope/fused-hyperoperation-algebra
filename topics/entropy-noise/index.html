Entropy is how far noise has pushed a qubit toward the wall

On the quantum noise page every fault channel was a point in a tetrahedron inside the cube, with total noise at the centre, the circle-operation wall. Entropy turns out to be distance from that centre, and different entropies measure it with different rulers: the cube, the sphere and the octahedron. All three touch at the same six points. Error correction is the work of pumping entropy back out, and the surface code's threshold sits just under half a bit per qubit.

Known or new. Every fact on this page is known physics or information theory: Tsallis' pseudo-additivity (1988), the hashing bound (Bennett, DiVincenzo, Smolin and Wootters, 1996), King's capacity formula for unital qubit channels (2002), the entanglement-breaking octahedron (Ruskai; Horodecki, Shor and Ruskai, 2003), Rényi entropy as free energy (Baez, 2011), the Nishimori line (1981) and its map to the surface code (Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl and Preskill, 2002).

What this page adds is the picture. Seen on the noise tetrahedron, three entropies are three norms of the same point λ, their unit shapes kiss at the same six points, and all three are absorbing regions around the circle-operation wall. I didn't find that stated in one place, but each piece is standard, so treat it as a tidy reading, not a discovery. Two other readings are tidy too: the Tsallis weight k = 1 − q is Carnot's efficiency 1 − T₀/T, and the 3-bit code's syndromes are the cube's four body diagonals.

Lead check. Nothing here is as strong as the KdV solitons. The nearest thing to a door is the half-bit threshold, below.

Tsallis entropy is the circle operation with dial k, with k = 1 − q

Tsallis entropy is Sq = (1 − Σpq)/(q − 1). Its lift is the power sum: 1 + k·S = Σpq with k = 1 − q. Power sums multiply for independent systems, so the lift multiplies, and that is exactly the circle operation ∘ₖ (called mult-add before 1.0) with weight k:

S(A + B) = S(A) + S(B) + (1 − q)·S(A)·S(B)

Checked on 1,400 random pairs of distributions and on random quantum states (Tr ρq), to 10−14. This is Tsallis' own 1988 law, and the information-theory page already proved it in Lean; this page reuses that proof.

Does it sit on the statistics line k + q = −1? No. The letter q means two different things. On the statistics page q is the landscape's curvature, the q in (a + b + k·ab)/(1 − q·ab). Tsallis laws have no denominator, so on the landscape they all sit at qL = 0. Lean proves that certainty swallows everything (a ⊕ 1 = 1 for every a) exactly when k = −1, so the Tsallis family meets the "or" line at one point only.

That point is Tsallis q = 2, k = −1: the collision entropy 1 − Σp², the chance two draws differ. It is the one entropy that is itself an "or" law ("the pair differs if A differs or B differs"), and it is the entropy that draws the sphere in the next section.

On the noise tetrahedron, three entropies are three rulers

A Pauli channel flips a qubit by X, Y or Z with chances pX, pY, pZ. Its point λ = (λx, λy, λz) says how much of each direction survives. The centre λ = 0 is total noise, the analogue of the circle operation's wall −1, and composing two channels multiplies the coordinates. Each entropy question about the channel turns out to depend on λ through one norm.

The sphere: collision entropy

Lean proves a Parseval identity on the tetrahedron: Σp² = (1 + |λ|²)/4. So the collision (Rényi-2) entropy of the fault distribution is constant on spheres around the centre. It is 0 at the four corners and largest at the centre.

The unit sphere is special. It is the tetrahedron's midsphere: it passes through the midpoints of all six edges. Inside it Σp² ≤ ½, so the collision entropy is at least one bit, and so is the Shannon entropy, which is never smaller. The hashing bound, the standard rate 1 − H(p) that a random code achieves, is then zero. Inside the midsphere, hashing gives nothing.

The plane: enough purity at a corner

The other side is a plane. If one fault chance is above 0.8107, H(p) is below one bit and hashing works. That number is where the depolarizing channel's hashing rate hits zero (total fault chance 0.18929). So the region where hashing works is sandwiched: it contains the four corner caps cut off by these planes and lies outside the midsphere. It touches the plane along the depolarizing lines and the sphere only at the edge midpoints, where one fault happens with chance exactly ½.

The octahedron: entanglement breaking

A channel is entanglement breaking, so useless for sending quantum information, exactly when |λx| + |λy| + |λz| ≤ 1 (Ruskai, 2003). That is the octahedron. Checked here on 5,000 random channels against the partial-transpose test of the channel's Choi state: all agree. Lean proves the octahedron sits inside the midsphere ball. Its six corners are the same six edge midpoints.

The cube: classical capacity

King (2002) showed the classical capacity of such a channel is 1 − h((1 − max|λᵢ|)/2), where h is the binary entropy. It depends only on the longest axis, so its level sets are cubes around the centre. Checked by minimizing the output von Neumann entropy over all pure inputs on 300 random channels. The unit cube's faces touch the sphere at the same six points.

The geometric statement. The ℓ∞ ball (classical capacity), the ℓ² ball (collision entropy) and the ℓ¹ ball (entanglement breaking) of the noise point λ all kiss at the tetrahedron's six edge midpoints. A channel there flips one way with chance ½: it carries one classical bit perfectly in one basis, and no quantum information.

The wall, fattened. In the circle operation, −1 absorbs everything. Here the centre does, and Lean proves each of the three balls absorbs too: compose a channel inside any of them with any other channel and it stays inside. Entropy can only move outward from the corners toward the wall.

The weight k is a temperature

For a thermal state at temperature T₀, Rényi entropy of order q is a free-energy difference between T₀ and T = T₀/q (Baez, 2011; checked to 10−15). The lift 1 + k·S is a ratio of partition functions, the same lift the thermodynamics page used. So the Tsallis weight reads

k = 1 − q = 1 − T₀/T

That is Carnot's efficiency between T and T₀. Carnot efficiencies cascade by the union rule, k = k₁ + k₂ − k₁k₂, and so do Tsallis weights along a chain of escort distributions (the escort of an escort multiplies the orders; Lean proves it for whole-number orders).

The landmarks line up with earlier pages. Infinite temperature is k = 1, the plain circle operation, counting states, which is also where the Lee–Yang squares became the plain circle-operation squares. T = T₀ is Shannon, plain addition. T = T₀/2 is the collision entropy and the union rule.

Stacking noise: cancellation hides entropy

Stack n bit-flip channels. The full fault pattern is a corner of the n-cube, and its entropy adds: n·h(p). But the qubit only feels the parity, the k = −2 rule from the noise page, and the net flip's entropy never goes past one bit. Everything else was in the even layers that cancelled.

Lean proves the purity version for every n: the net channel's collision sum (1 + λ2n)/2 is always at least the pattern's ((1 + λ²)/2)n. At n = 2 the gap is a perfect square, (1 − λ²)²/4. The pattern's linear entropy climbs the cube at weight −1, rung 2.

Error correction is entropy removal, and the cube shows where it goes

The 3-bit code measures two parities. Lean checks all 64 pairs of corners: the four possible syndromes split the 3-cube into its four body diagonals, each a pair of opposite corners. The syndrome says which diagonal you are on; the vote picks the lighter end.

So the fault entropy splits exactly: H(faults) = H(syndrome) + H(left on the diagonal). The syndrome removes most of it; what is left is the logical error. Lean proves the chain rule for any log-like function, and the syndrome chances in the polarization: (1 + 3λ²)/4 for no alarm, (1 − λ²)/4 for each alarm.

One exact surprise: after an alarm, the leftover doubt is exactly one fresh coin of chance p. The two ends of an alarmed diagonal have weights p(1 − p)² and p²(1 − p), so the code turns a three-coin problem into a one-coin problem. Concatenation then runs the flow from the noise page, and the leftover entropy falls every level below the wall.

Thresholds as an entropy budget

For bit-flips on the surface code, the best possible decoder works below about 10.93%, and the matching decoder from the noise page below about 10.3%. Both sit just under the point where one qubit's flip entropy reaches half a bit: h(p) = ½ at p = 0.1100.

That is not a coincidence of this page. Random CSS codes reach rate 1 − 2h(p), which is positive exactly below 0.1100, and Nishimori and Nemoto's duality argument for the square lattice predicts the same 0.1100. The real threshold is a little lower, so the half-bit rule is a close estimate, not an exact law.

The Nishimori link to the thermodynamics page. The surface code's threshold maps onto a random magnet on the Nishimori line, where the coupling K satisfies e−2K = p/(1 − p). Lean proves that this makes tanh K = 1 − 2p = λ, the bit-flip polarization. So the noise page's k = −2 rule and the thermodynamics page's Ising bonds in series are one law, and the Kramers–Wannier lens (1 − t)/(1 + t) sends λ to the error odds p/(1 − p).

Heat. Landauer: erasing a bit costs at least kT·ln 2 of heat. At p = 0.1% each qubit gives up 0.011 bits a round, about 2·10−27 J at 20 mK. Tiny, but it is the floor for running any code.

What Lean proves, and what it doesn't

lean/EntropyNoise.lean: 47 theorems in core Lean 4.37.0-rc1, no mathlib, no sorry, each using only Lean's standard axioms (list in lean/theorems.tsv). It imports the shared library's Base, QuantumNoise and InformationTheory modules and is ready to go in as FusedAlgebra/Topics/EntropyNoise.lean. Of the 47, 9 are small order and algebra helpers and 4 restate library theorems under entropy names, which leaves 34 new results.

  • The weight: certainty absorbs exactly at k = −1; weights compose by the union rule; Carnot's 1 − T₀/T cascades; escorts of escorts multiply their orders.
  • The tetrahedron: Parseval Σp² = (1 + |λ|²)/4; linear entropy (3 − |λ|²)/4; Σp² ≤ ½ iff inside the midsphere; corner, centre and edge-midpoint values; octahedron inside the ball inside the cube; all three absorb under composition; collision sums in series and in parallel; the depolarizing diagonal.
  • Stacking: the pattern's linear entropy climbs at weight −1; the net channel is purer for every n; the n = 2 gap is a square.
  • The 3-bit code: syndromes are body diagonals; syndrome chances in λ; the vote's failure is the cube's top two layers; an alarm leaves one fresh coin; the chain rule for any log-like L.
  • Nishimori: tanh K = 1 − 2p; the Kramers–Wannier lens sends λ to p/(1 − p); decimation is the bit-flip law.

Not in Lean, because core Lean has no real numbers or logarithms: Shannon and von Neumann entropy as numbers, the hashing bound and its 0.18929 point, the 0.8107 plane, King's formula, that the octahedron is exactly the entanglement-breaking set, Baez's identity, and h(p) = ½ at 0.1100. All of these are in checks.py (45 checks, output in checks_output.txt). The surface-code thresholds 0.103 and 0.1093 are from the literature and the noise page's simulation, not re-run here.
