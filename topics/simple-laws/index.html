The landscape from the ground up

Early science lands on only a handful of spots on the map: addition, the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0), the failure line to the left of it, the turning corner and the speed-of-light corner. And geometry falls out on its own: every point of the plane is the circle operation in its own number system, and the three countries are Euclid's, Galileo's and Einstein's geometry.

    Every point of the map is the circle operation in its own number system

    Invent a new unit u with one rule, u² = k·u − q. Numbers are now a + b·u. Combine two "tilted ones" by the circle operation:

    (1 + x·u)(1 + y·u) = (1 − q·x·y) + (x + y + k·x·y)·u

    Read the answer as a slope, the u part over the plain part, and you get exactly the law at the point (k, q) of the landscape. So every law on the map is the circle operation, done in a different number system and read as a slope.

    Each number system comes with its own Pythagoras. The size a² + k·ab + q·b² multiplies when the numbers multiply, so it is a length. What kind of length depends on the country:

    Turning: Euclid

    u is like i. The length is a² + b² in disguise, its unit circle is a circle, and moving along the law is rotation. Trigonometry lives here.

    Border: Galileo

    u² = 0 after a shift. The length is one square, so one direction has no length at all. Galileo's velocity addition, plain x + y, is the corner of this country.

    Two walls: Einstein

    u splits into two real directions. The length is a² − b², its unit circle is a hyperbola, and the walls are its asymptotes: the light cone.

    The circle operation's own point

    At (1, 0) the unit is u² = u. The length is (1 + x)(1 + y), and its unit circle, (1 + x)(1 + y) = 1, is Boyle's hyperbola PV = constant. Its asymptotes are the walls at −1.

    Proved in Lean: the slope law at every (k, q), the length multiplying, the three ways the length factors, and the unit circle at the circle operation's point (NumberPlane.lean, 26 theorems). Known as Clifford's and Study's number systems; Yaglom's A Simple Non-Euclidean Geometry and Its Physical Basis (1979) tells the Galileo and Einstein story with them. New here is reading the whole landscape plane through them.

    Lead check: no new soliton, but the geometry now comes from the circle operation

    The biggest result on this page is the number-system reading. It answers your rule that the geometry must be derived from the circle operation: one rule for u gives each point of the map its own length, and the three countries come out as Euclid, Galileo and Einstein, with Boyle's hyperbola as the circle operation's own circle. It is known mathematics (Clifford, Study, Yaglom), so it is a foundation, not a discovery like a new law.

    Nothing in early science sits off the five familiar spots except the law of cosines, which draws a whole line, q = 1. Every real triangle lies inside the turning country on that line, flat triangles sit on the border, and past the border cosine becomes cosh: the triangles of relativity. A rotation seen in axes tilted to angle θ is exactly the law at (2 cos θ, 1) (checked numerically to 10⁻¹⁰).

    Next door worth opening: the same number systems in three or four dimensions (quaternions and their cousins) would put the circle-operation cube's higher layers on the map, which is where the tesseract coordinates could plug in.
