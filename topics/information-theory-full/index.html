In information theory the dial's weight is what you count

The earlier information page showed that failure chances, Tsallis entropy, Kraft sums and channel cascades all compose by a + b + k·ab. This full pass asks where k comes from. Grow a decision tree with D answers per question and k = D − 1. Take a Tsallis entropy of index qT and k = 1 − qT. Chain any two-input channel and k = −1. The q-logarithm turns out to be the dial's own logarithm, so these are one fact read three ways.

Tree A (navy) asks its questions; then tree B is grafted onto every leaf. The first copy of B (cyan) adds B's questions once. Every other copy (amber) is the corner: there are (D − 1)·IA of them, each with IB questions.

Questions in the grafted tree

IA∘B = IA + IB + (D − 1)·IA·IB

Leaves are the lift: a full D-ary tree with I questions has 1 + (D − 1)·I leaves, and leaves multiply when you graft. So questions compose by the dial with k = D − 1, and a complete tree of depth n asks [n]D = 1 + D + ⋯ + Dn−1 questions, a q-number in base D = k + 1.

Counting decisions is the root: grafting trees is the circle operation

Hartley's count (1928) says that choosing among N outcomes takes log N units; the earlier page noted that N − 1 composes by the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0). Trees show why. Asking every question of A and then every question of B is grafting B onto each leaf of A. Leaves multiply (that is Hartley), and leaves = 1 + (D − 1)·questions, so the question count rides on the lift. Lean proves the binary case on actual trees, by structural induction, and the D-ary case on the counts.

Two side facts drop out. Every full tree has Kraft sum exactly 1 (each leaf at depth ℓ weighs D−ℓ), and grafting keeps it 1, the tree form of the Kraft product on the earlier page. And the complete tree of depth n asks [n]D questions: this is where the project's q-numbers meet information, with q equal to the number of answers.

The q-logarithm is the dial's own logarithm

Tsallis' q-logarithm is lnqx = (x1−q − 1)/(1 − q). Write k = 1 − q (the Tsallis index, labelled qT on the other pages) and it is (xk − 1)/k: raise to the power k, then undo the dial's lift. So it turns products into the dial:

lnq(xy) = lnqx ∘k lnqy, eq(u ∘k v) = eq(u)·eq(v)

with the q-exponential eq(u) = (1 + ku)1/k as its inverse. At k = 1 it is x − 1, the count minus one. At k = −1 it is 1 − 1/x, which obeys the union rule. At k = 0 it is the ordinary log. Lean proves the law for every whole-number weight, the k = −1 case, and the inverse.

Tsallis entropy is an average, and the average passes through the dial

Call s(p) = lnq(1/p) the q-surprise of an outcome. Tsallis entropy is its average, Σ p·s(p), and that equals (Σpq − 1)/(1 − q). For independent sources the surprise of a joint outcome is s(p) ∘k s(r), because the q-log turns the product pr into the dial.

The measure and probability page of this pass proves that for independent quantities E[X ∘k Y] = E X ∘k E Y. Apply it to the surprises and Tsallis' 1988 law follows in one line: S(A×B) = S(A) ∘k S(B). Lean proves this as one lemma for any surprise that turns products into ∘k; at k = 0 it is the earlier page's outer-log lemma for Shannon. For correlated sources the miss is exactly k·Cov of the two surprises.

Two q's, honestly

Before 1.0 the project read the dial as q-numbers in base k + 1, while Tsallis writes k = 1 − qT. So the base is 2 − qT. That is not a clash to hide: q ↔ 2 − q is Tsallis statistics' own known "additive duality", ln2−q(x) = −lnq(1/x), and on the dial it is the sign flip k → −k. Lean proves the dual log composes with weight −k. Read a Tsallis index through the project's eyes by flipping it around 1; the pages now state the dial in k and label the Tsallis index qT.

The Sharma–Mittal plane: order α across, dial weight k = 1 − β up. Each horizontal row composes by one dial. Teal: Rényi, k = 0, adds. Amber: Tsallis, k = 1 − α. Dashed: Landsberg–Vedral, k = α − 1, its flip.

Every composable entropy here is Rényi pushed through a dial

Sharma and Mittal (1975) found a two-index family that contains Shannon, Rényi and Tsallis. Through the lift it has a one-line description: take Rényi entropy of order α, which adds over independent sources, and push it through the dial's exponential (ekx − 1)/k.

Hα,β = (e(1−β)·Rényiα − 1)/(1 − β), and H(A×B) = H(A) ∘k H(B), k = 1 − β

So the order α says which average you take, and the weight k says which dial carries it. Tsallis is the diagonal β = α; Landsberg and Vedral's normalized entropy (1998) is β = 2 − α, the flipped dial. Checked on 300 random pairs.

The q-exponential is compound interest, not the rung flow

At k = 1/n the q-exponential is (1 + x/n)n: n circle-operation steps of x/n, the climb up the cube toward e. It runs from the identity (k = 1) to ex − 1 (k = 0), so it looked like a fractional rung flow. It is not one: two half-steps match ex − 1 through x² and miss at x³ (1/8 against 1/6). It interpolates between rungs without being a flow.

A two-input channel as a parallelogram: its rows (1 − a, a) and (b, 1 − b) span an area 1 − (a + b). Chain two channels and the areas multiply, so the crossover sum composes by the union rule. Shown: s₁ = 0.3, s₂ = 0.4, cascade 0.58.

Any binary channel chains by the union rule

The earlier page had the symmetric channel (parity, k = −2) and the alphabet ladder. Here is the general two-input case: whatever a = P(1 | 0) and b = P(0 | 1) are, the crossover sum s = a + b of a cascade is s₁ ∘₋₁ s₂, because the determinant 1 − s multiplies. The symmetric channel has s = 2p, and the union rule on 2p is the parity rule on p. The Z-channel has s = p and chains by the union rule directly; its capacity falls every hop (0.504, 0.314, 0.206, … bits for s = 0.3).

Lead worth following?

No new lead on its own merits. Every law here is known, and the full pass mostly confirms that the earlier page's picture was complete: wherever information composes, it composes by a dial, and the weight is fixed by what is being counted.

What the pass adds is a cleaner account of where each weight comes from. The number of answers per question gives k = D − 1, so D = k + 1 is the alphabet size. The Tsallis index gives k = 1 − qT, which is D flipped around 1 by a known duality. And the general Tsallis law is no longer a separate fact: it is the measure page's "expectation passes through the dial", applied to surprise. That makes information theory a derived branch of the measure picture, which is the order these two pages follow.

It predicts nothing new, so I would not spend more time here unless a bent landscape law turns out to describe some real coding cost, the way Campbell's exponential cost matches the dial.
