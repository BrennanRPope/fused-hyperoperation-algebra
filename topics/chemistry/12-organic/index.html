The rings and Hammett parts are textbook. Nothing here predicts something new, so I didn't follow it further.

Closing the chain: Hückel and Möbius rings, and the 4n + 2 rule

Subject 4 found open Hückel chains on the landscape. Closing the chain into a ring gives levels 2cos(2πj/N) in units of β; putting a half-twist in the ring (a Möbius ring) shifts them to 2cos((2j + 1)π/N). The characteristic polynomials are 2TN(x/2) − 2 and 2TN(x/2) + 2, with TN the Chebyshev polynomial, checked for N = 3 to 10.

Filling the levels gives the 4n + 2 rule. Benzene has π energy 8β, 2β more than three ethylenes. Cyclobutadiene gains nothing and leaves two electrons in a degenerate pair. A Möbius four-ring reverses this: levels ±√2, π energy 5.66β and a full gap. Rings with 6 or 10 electrons are closed-shell as Hückel rings; rings with 4, 8 or 12 are closed-shell as Möbius rings (picture at the top). This is standard Hückel and Heilbronner theory, now checked and proved for the small rings.

Hammett: σ adds, so rate ratios multiply

Hammett's rule log(k/k₀) = ρσ makes substituent constants add (the k = 0 law), so the rate ratios of two substituents multiply and k/k₀ − 1 composes by the circle operation (called mult-add before 1.0). This is the same log-of-the-lift fact as Gibbs energies and pKas, and it adds nothing beyond them.

Selectivity: ee composes by the velocity-addition law, the landscape point (0, −1)

An enantiomer ratio r gives ee = (r − 1)/(r + 1), which is tanh of half of ln r, so ee is a hyperbolic tangent of the free-energy difference. When two selective steps act together in the matched sense their ratios multiply, so

ee = (e₁ + e₂)/(1 + e₁e₂)

which is the landscape law at (k, q) = (0, −1), and also Einstein's addition of velocities with ee playing v/c. It sits in the two-walls country, with walls at ee = ±1: a perfectly selective step absorbs anything. A 10:1 step (ee 0.818) matched with a 5:1 step (ee 0.667) gives 0.961; mismatched they give 0.333.

Horeau's duplication is the same law applied to itself, e ⊕ e = 2e/(1 + e²): 20% ee becomes 38%, 67%, 92% and 99.7% in four rounds. Free energy plays the role of rapidity, so each extra nine of ee costs close to RT ln 10 = 5.7 kJ/mol at room temperature (7.3, 13.1 and 18.8 kJ/mol for 90%, 99% and 99.9%).

ee of a matched pair of selective steps against the second step's ee, for four values of the first step's ee. The curves follow the velocity-addition law and all bend toward the wall at ee = 1.

Yields: losses fold at k = −1, and only the longest branch counts

A linear route's losses compose by failure-union, as in subject 5: six steps at 90, 85, 95, 80, 90 and 70% lose 64% overall. Eight 90% steps in a line keep 43%, while two parallel four-step branches joined by a 90% coupling keep 59%, because only the longest branch counts. This is the familiar case for convergent synthesis.
