Lead check: not a lead

The triprotic test is clean and decisive, but it goes against the dial: its many-site fold does not describe polyprotic acids, and the picture that fits is classical pairwise electrostatics. The rest restates known log and mixing rules. I noted the negative result and didn't follow it further.

pH turns the lift into addition, and independent groups fold at k = −1

Chaining dissociation steps multiplies their constants, so pKa values add and K − 1 composes by the circle operation (called mult-add before 1.0), as in subject 7. The fraction of an acid deprotonated is a logistic curve in pH (Henderson–Hasselbalch). For two independent groups, the chance that at least one has lost its proton is α₁ ⊞₋₁ α₂, the failure-union law again.

The isoelectric point is an average, not a circle operation: glycine (pKa 2.34 and 9.60) is neutral at pH 5.97, exactly their mean, because h² = K₁K₂ there.

Buffers: capacities add, and mixing combines ratios by the mediant

Two buffers in one solution have a total capacity equal to the sum of their separate terms at the shared pH, the plain addition law (k = 0). Mixing two buffers made from one acid combines their base-to-acid ratios as the mediant (b₁ + b₂)/(a₁ + a₂), which always lies between the two. The full equilibrium calculation agrees with the mediant formula to 0.001 pH units in the test case. Neither of these is the circle operation.

A real test: triprotic acids follow pairwise interactions, not the dial folded over three sites

Subject 8 showed that folding the dial over n sites gives the MWC model with exclusive binding. For three sites that fold makes the protonation polynomial 1 + 3x + 3kx² + k²x³, and a short calculation shows that this pins the lower gap pKa₂ − pKa₁ at log 3 = 0.48, whatever k is. A uniform interaction between every pair of protons instead gives 1 + 3x + 3kx² + k³x³, which makes the two gaps equal.

Real acids pick the second. From one standard table: citric acid has gaps 1.63 and 1.64, phosphoric 5.06 and 5.14, arsenic 4.72 and 4.59 (arsenic values vary by about 0.2 between sources). Each pair is close to equal and every gap is far from 0.48. Fitting the pairwise factor gives about 6.6 kJ/mol of repulsion per proton pair for citric acid and 26 kJ/mol for phosphoric. Using the first two pKas to predict the third gives 6.39 for citric (table 6.40), 12.26 for phosphoric (12.34) and 11.65 for arsenic (11.52).

So the dial's many-site fold, which matched MWC exactly, does not describe polyprotic acids. That is a useful negative: it marks where the fold stops applying. The pairwise picture that does fit is the classical Bjerrum and Kirkwood–Westheimer electrostatic view.
