Lead check: a sturdy picture, not a lead to new results

The angle picture is exact and geometric, and it ties Lavoisier, atomic binding and free collisions into one law of cosines; it predicts hydrogen's ionisation energy to 2.5 parts per million from α alone. But it is known physics reparametrised: θ = Zα is the Coulomb binding energy written as an angle. I found no prediction it makes that the usual formulas don't. Its value for the project is the map it gives the rest of the chemistry pass: bound means turning, free means two walls.

Invariant mass is a law of cosines, and Lavoisier sits on the border

Two parts with masses m₁ and m₂ make a whole whose mass obeys

M² = m₁² + m₂² + 2 m₁ m₂ c

This is the law of cosines, the line d = 1 on the landscape. The value of c decides the country:

  • c = 1, the border. M = m₁ + m₂ exactly: Lavoisier's conservation of mass. The triangle is flat.
  • c = cos θ < 1, turning. A bound state: the whole weighs less than its parts, and θ is a real angle.
  • c = cosh η > 1, two walls. Two free particles flying apart: the pair weighs more, and η is their relative rapidity, the ruler from the relativity page.

So binding is a rotation and free motion is a boost, the same split the relativity and early-laws pages found between Euclid's and Einstein's countries. Lean proves the bookkeeping: bound pairs weigh less, free pairs weigh more, and the binding energy B satisfies B(2(m₁ + m₂) − B) = 2m₁m₂(1 − c) exactly.

The mass of a pair of equal parts, as a fraction of the parts' sum, across the three countries. Left of the border the pair is bound by angle θ; right of it the parts fly apart with rapidity η.

Hydrogen's binding angle is the fine-structure constant

Measure the angle for real bound states and the Coulomb ones line up on one value. Hydrogen's 1s state gives θ = 7.297408e-03, against α = 7.297353e-03: they agree to 8 parts per million. Positronium and muonium, with very different masses, give the same angle. He⁺ gives 2α. To leading order θ = √(2B/μ), and a Coulomb binding energy is ½μ(Zα)², so θ = Zα for every hydrogen-like system, whatever its masses.

Taking sin θ = α exactly, the compact two-body Dirac form, predicts hydrogen's ionisation energy as 13.598468 eV against the measured 13.598434 eV. The difference, 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ eV, is the 1s Lamb shift (a quantum-electrodynamics correction) to within 2%.

Chemistry sits much nearer the border: an H₂ bond has θ ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻⁴, so burning hydrogen loses only 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ of the water's mass. Lavoisier's balances, good to about one part in ten thousand, could never see it. That is why conservation of mass is exact for chemists and approximate for physicists: chemistry lives within a hair of the border.

Charge is the k = 0 law, and its phase is the circle operation on the circle

Charges add with no corner: the k = 0 line, the circle operation's own identity line. Their exponentials multiply. The phase eiQφ of a charge Q, or the fugacity zQ in a grand ensemble, multiplies as charges add, so its excess combines by the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0): ei(Q₁+Q₂)φ − 1 = (eiQ₁φ − 1) ∘₁ (eiQ₂φ − 1). On the unit circle that is rotation, the turning country again.

Every additive conservation law in chemistry has this shape: the conserved quantity is the log of a lift whose excess combines by the circle operation. A balanced redox equation such as MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O balances atoms and charge (+17 on each side) by plain addition.
