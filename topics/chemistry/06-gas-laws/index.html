Lead check: not a lead

The van der Waals factorization is exact, but it is van der Waals' own product form divided through, so it predicts nothing new. The Mayer reading is exact and is what the cluster expansion already is. The cubic-equation placement is formal.

van der Waals' own equation is a circle operation (called mult-add before 1.0) of attraction and excluded volume

Van der Waals wrote his law as a product, (P + a/V²)(V − b) = RT. Divide both sides by RT·V and it says, with no approximation,

1/Z = (1 + α)(1 − β), α = a/PV², β = b/V

so 1/Z − 1 = α ∘₁ (−β): the attraction correction composed by the circle operation with minus the excluded-volume correction. Checked for five gases in four states each, and proved in Lean from the equation itself.

The corner −αβ is not a rounding term. For CO₂ at 300 K and 0.2 L/mol, α = 1.35, β = 0.21 and the corner is -0.29. To first order the two corrections simply add, which is why the second virial coefficient is b − a/RT. The corner first enters at the third virial coefficient.

The three pieces of 1/Z − 1 for CO₂ at 350 K. At large volume α and −β nearly cancel; squeezed, the corner grows to a real share.

The Boltzmann factor is the circle-operation lift, and Mayer's clusters are its corners

For particles with pair energies u, the Boltzmann factor is a product over pairs of e−u/kT = 1 + f. A product of (1 + f)'s is the lift of a circle operation fold, so

Π (1 + fij) = 1 + (f₁ ∘₁ f₂ ∘₁ … ∘₁ fm)

and expanding the fold gives one term for each nonempty set of pairs, 2m − 1 in all. Those sets are Mayer's cluster graphs, so the cluster expansion is the circle operation's corner expansion. Lean proves the three-pair case term by term and the general list version.

The first term gives the second virial coefficient. For argon with a Lennard-Jones potential it changes sign at 409 K, within half a percent of the 408 K from the 2023 ab initio argon potential (the picture at the top).

The cubic equations of state differ only in a quadratic denominator, placed here for the record

Redlich–Kwong and Peng–Robinson keep van der Waals' repulsion and change the attraction's denominator from V² to V² + u·bV + w·b²: (0, 0) for van der Waals, (1, 0) for Redlich–Kwong and Soave, and (2, −1) for Peng–Robinson. The discriminants are 0, 1 and 8. Their critical compressibilities come out at 0.375, 0.333 and 0.307, moving toward the real value near 0.29.

A quadratic form's discriminant is what sorts the landscape's three countries, so van der Waals sits on the border and the other two in the two-walls country. That placement is formal: I found no physics in it beyond the known fact that a third parameter (Patel–Teja and others) is needed to match Zc.

Critical compressibility of the three classic cubic equations against the discriminant of their attraction denominator, with real gases near 0.29.

At Haber conditions the gases are nearly ideal, and the corrections multiply

For nitrogen fixation the gas laws matter at the Haber process's 700 K and 200 bar. Van der Waals gives fugacity coefficients of 1.061 for N₂, 1.081 for H₂ and 0.888 for NH₃. They enter the equilibrium as a product, φNH₃²/(φN₂φH₂³) = 0.59, so their logarithms add: the lift once more. On this van der Waals estimate, non-ideality raises the ammonia yield at a given K by about 70%. Equilibrium itself comes in subject 8.
