Lead check: not a lead

The k = −1 law is exactly right for yields and for heavy-isotope odds, but chemists already work with products of yields and with isotope generating functions, so writing them as the circle operation changes no calculation. Proportions and balancing are counting and linear algebra with no the circle operation in them.

Yields multiply, so losses the circle operation at k = −1

A step that keeps a fraction y of the material loses ℓ = 1 − y. Two steps keep y₁y₂, so their losses combine by the weighted circle operation at k = −1, the same failure-union law as two independent risks:

ℓ₁ ⊞₋₁ ℓ₂ = ℓ₁ + ℓ₂ − ℓ₁ℓ₂ = 1 − (1 − ℓ₁)(1 − ℓ₂)

The fold is associative and order-free, so n equal steps lose the k = −1 power 1 − (1 − ℓ)ⁿ. Ten steps at 90% each keep only 34.9%.

What the law says about planning is that only the number of steps a molecule passes through matters, not the total number of steps. Joining eight fragments one at a time puts the first fragment through seven couplings, 0.8⁷ = 21% at 80% each. Joining them in pairs, then pairs of pairs, puts every fragment through three, 0.8³ = 51%. That is the standard case for convergent synthesis, and it is just the depth of the fold.

A molecule's isotope pattern is the product of its atoms' patterns

Write each atom's isotopes as a polynomial in a mass-step x: chlorine is 0.758 + 0.242x². A molecule's pattern is the product of its atoms' polynomials, so patterns compose by the lift. Two chlorines give (3 + x)² ≈ 9 : 6 : 1 and two bromines (1 + x)² ≈ 1 : 2 : 1, the peaks every mass spectrometrist knows.

The k = −1 law shows up again in the chance that a molecule carries at least one heavy atom, 1 − (1 − p)ⁿ. For C₆₀ that is 47.6%, and the M+1 peak is 64.9% of the M peak.

Whole-number ratios, and mixed oxides are mediants

Dalton's law of multiple proportions holds to the precision of the atomic masses: the grams of oxygen per gram of nitrogen in N₂O, NO, N₂O₃, NO₂ and N₂O₅ stand exactly 1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : 5.

The definite proportions of a mixed compound follow the mediant rule, because combining two compounds adds their atom counts. Magnetite Fe₃O₄ is FeO plus Fe₂O₃, so its iron-to-oxygen ratio 3 : 4 is (1 + 2) : (1 + 3), which lies between 1 : 1 and 2 : 3. The same holds for red lead Pb₃O₄ = 2PbO + PbO₂ and hausmannite Mn₃O₄. Lean proves the mediant always lies between its parents. These are counting facts, not compose by the circle operation.

Magnetite's iron content sits between its two parent oxides, as the mediant of their Fe : O ratios must.

Balancing an equation means finding the one direction the element counts leave free

Write each species' element counts as a column, reactants positive and products negative. A balanced equation is a vector that sums the columns to zero. For the Haber process, respiration and photosynthesis there is exactly one such direction, giving N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ and C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ ⇌ 6CO₂ + 6H₂O.

Nitrogenase is the instructive case. N₂ + H → NH₃ + H₂ leaves a two-way family of balanced equations, so element counts alone cannot say how much hydrogen the enzyme wastes. The measured one H₂ per N₂ is a fact about its mechanism, and it comes back in subject 13.
