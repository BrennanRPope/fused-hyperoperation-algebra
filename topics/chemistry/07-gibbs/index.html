Lead check: a useful reading, not yet a lead

The dial gets a plain physical meaning here, the Boltzmann factor of an interaction, with addition and the circle operation as its exclusion and independence settings. That is exact and it organizes the next subjects, but on two sites it is ordinary statistical mechanics. Whether it says anything new depends on many sites, which subject 8 tests.

Free energies add, so equilibrium constants multiply: K is the lift side

When two reactions are added, their free energies add and their equilibrium constants multiply, because K = e−ΔG/RT. In the circle operation terms the excess K − 1 combines by the circle operation, (K₁ − 1) ∘₁ (K₂ − 1) = K₁K₂ − 1, exactly as for any quantity whose logarithm adds.

This is how cells run uphill reactions. Making glutamine from glutamate and ammonium costs +14.2 kJ/mol (K′ = 3.3e-03). Coupled to ATP hydrolysis at −30.5 kJ/mol (K′ = 2.2e+05), the combined step has K′ = 717. The energies add and the constants multiply.

The dial is the Boltzmann factor of an interaction

This is where the dial shows up as a physical quantity. Take two binding sites with weights x and y (concentration times affinity), and let a pair of occupants interact with energy ε, so the doubly-occupied state carries an extra factor ω = e−ε/RT. The partition function is

Z = 1 + x + y + ω·xy, so Z − 1 = x ⊞ω y

which is the weighted circle operation with the dial set to ω. The three familiar cases are the three special settings:

  • k = 0: hard exclusion. Only one site can be filled, Z = 1 + x + y, and the occupations simply add.
  • k = 1: no interaction. Z = (1 + x)(1 + y), the sites are independent, and the law is the circle operation.
  • k > 1: attraction, so the occupants help each other (positive cooperativity).

The dial's distance from 1 is the correlation between the sites: the covariance of the two occupancies is exactly xy(k − 1)/Z², which Lean proves. At room temperature a 5 kJ/mol repulsion sets k = 0.13, and an equal attraction sets k = 7.5. Equilibrium (next subject) takes this to many sites.

Making ammonia is downhill at room temperature and uphill where it is made

From standard tables, N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ has ΔH° = -91.8 kJ and ΔS° = -198.1 J/K. At 298 K, ΔG° = -32.7 kJ, matching twice the tabulated −16.4 kJ/mol for ammonia, and K ≈ 5.4e+05. The reaction gives off heat but loses entropy (four gas molecules become two), so ΔG° crosses zero near 463 K in the constant-ΔH approximation. At the 700 K of a Haber reactor, K has fallen to about 3e-04 per bar².

So thermodynamics wants nitrogen fixed cold, and the reason for running hot is the rate (subject 9): the N≡N bond's hard first step (subject 4) is too slow at room temperature even on iron. The high pressure pushes the equilibrium back (subject 8).
