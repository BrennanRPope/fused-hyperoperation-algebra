Lead check: not a lead

Everything here checks out, but each piece is either a restatement (K − 1 composes by the circle operation, losses fold at k = −1) or a known result (Luther's rule, the 35.6 mV statistical spacing, Kok's model). The two-site dial is the same structure as subject 8 and adds nothing beyond it. I didn't follow any of it further.

ΔG adds and K multiplies, but potentials are averaged, not composed by the circle operation

Coupling two half-reactions adds their ΔG = −nFE, so their equilibrium constants multiply and K − 1 composes by the circle operation, exactly as in subject 7. The same holds for the forward/backward rate ratio of each step, eA/RT: in series the ratios multiply, so ratio − 1 composes by the circle operation (De Donder's relation).

The potentials themselves do something else. Combining two couples gives the electron-weighted mean, Luther's rule E = (n₁E₁ + n₂E₂)/(n₁ + n₂). From standard tables, Cu²⁺/Cu⁺ (0.153 V) and Cu⁺/Cu (0.521 V) give 0.337 V for Cu²⁺/Cu (tables list 0.342), and iron gives -0.041 V (tables list −0.037). A mean is idempotent, a couple combined with itself returns its own potential, so this is averaging, not compose by the circle operation. On a Frost diagram it is the slope rule, and a middle state above the chord disproportionates: Cu⁺ does, with K ≈ 1.7e+06.

Butler–Volmer current is the circle operation in the overpotential only in the one-sided limit α = 1, where i/i₀ = eFη/RT − 1. At α = ½ the gap is large (-1.72 in the test case); only the forward/backward ratio composes. The Tafel slope there is 118 mV per decade.

Two redox sites: Z − 1 = x ⊞ₖ x, and 35.6 mV marks independence

A molecule with two one-electron sites has the electron binding polynomial Z = 1 + 2x + kx², with x the electron activity and k the interaction factor. So Z − 1 = x ⊞ₖ x, the dial again. Matching it to the two measured potentials gives E₁ − E₂ = (RT/F) ln(4/k):

  • k = 1, independent sites: the potentials sit 35.6 mV apart, purely from counting.
  • k > 1, cooperative: ΔE < 35.6 mV, the Nernst curve is steeper than one electron, and Z has complex zeros.
  • k > 4, inverted: the second electron goes on more easily than the first.

The midpoint steepness is n = 2√k/(1 + √k). This is subject 8's diacid with electrons in place of protons (there k = 4·10−ΔpKa; here k = 4/Kc, the comproportionation constant). The 35.6 mV statistical spacing is well known in electrochemistry.

Fraction of electrons taken up by a two-site molecule against potential, for three interaction factors. Small k gives two separate one-electron waves; k = 1 is the independent case; large k merges them into one steep two-electron wave.

Photosynthesis: losses compose at k = −1, the Z-scheme spends two photons per electron, and Kok's clock is a circulant

Any chain of stages loses energy by failure-union: total loss = L₁ ⊞₋₁ L₂ ⊞₋₁ …, the same law as yields in subject 5. The published ceilings for converting sunlight into biomass are 4.6% for C3 and 6.0% for C4 plants, or 9.4% and 12.3% of photosynthetically active light; both pairs imply that about 49% of sunlight is usable, so they are consistent.

The Z-scheme uses 8 photons per O₂ (4 at 680 nm and 4 at 700 nm, 14.4 eV) to lift 4 electrons from water (+0.815 V) to NADP⁺ (−0.320 V), storing 31.6%. Thermodynamics alone would allow one 680 nm photon per electron (62%), so the scheme, not the energy, sets the cost.

Kok's water-splitting clock advances 0, 1 or 2 steps per flash (misses α, double hits β). Its transition matrix is circulant, so the oscillation decays by |α − β + i(1 − α − β)| per flash, 0.851 for α = 10%, β = 5% (picture at the top), and settles at (1 − α + β)/4 O₂ per flash. Composing flashes multiplies the one-flash generating function, which is the lift, but that is all it is.
