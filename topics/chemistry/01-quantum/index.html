Size-consistency is mult-add: CISD fails because it drops the corner

Two molecules that don't interact have a wavefunction equal to the product of theirs, so their excited parts compose by mult-add. Truncated CI keeps only the edges and loses the corner; coupled cluster keeps it by working in the log of the lift.

Lead check: not a lead

Size-consistency is fully understood in quantum chemistry: it is why coupled cluster replaced truncated CI decades ago. Mult-add explains it in one line, which makes this the clearest teaching picture of the failure, but it adds no prediction. The one small new fact, that a partial corner weight still fails, follows directly from the algebra.

Two molecules that ignore each other: their wavefunctions multiply

Write a molecule's wavefunction as the reference determinant times 1 + c, where c holds every excited configuration (intermediate normalisation). For two molecules too far apart to interact, the exact wavefunction is the product, so

1 + cAB = (1 + cA)(1 + cB)   ⟹   cAB = cA ✳ cB = cA + cB + cAcB

The corner cAcB is "both molecules excited at the same time". For two two-electron molecules that is a quadruple excitation of the pair.

Truncated configuration interaction with singles and doubles (CISD) keeps only excitations up to two electrons, so it keeps cA + cB and throws the corner away. It is the weighted law at k = 0. That is the whole reason CISD is not size-consistent. Coupled cluster writes 1 + c = eT: T is the log of the lift, so T adds and the corner comes back by itself. In mult-add terms, coupled cluster is the ruler that turns ✳ into +.

Three fragments A, B, C on the mult-add cube. CISD keeps the red edges out of the reference corner (one fragment excited at a time). Every blue corner is a product of fragment excitations that only the full ✳ keeps.

With N molecules, CISD keeps a share that falls like 1/√N

The checks file solves a model exactly: N identical molecules, each with a reference state and one doubly excited state 2Δ above it, coupled by g (Δ = 1, g = 0.3). The exact energy is N times one molecule. CISD's energy is Δ − √(Δ² + N g²), so its share of the correlation falls like 1/√N: 98% for two molecules, 86% for ten, under 7% for ten thousand.

A wavefunction that keeps the corner with weight k (every m-fold product scaled by km−1) is exact only at k = 1. Half a corner is better than none but still loses ground as N grows. Size-consistency is not a matter of degree: only the full mult-add composes correctly.

Lean proves the model's key step: if e₁ solves one molecule, then N·e₁ misses the N-molecule CISD equation by exactly N(N − 1)e₁², the count of molecule pairs times the corner (cisd_gap, cisd_not_extensive).

Real molecules show the same loss: 0.7 kcal/mol for two H₂, 5.9 for two waters

I ran PySCF on real molecules 100 Å apart, where they cannot interact.

  • Two H₂ (cc-pVDZ): CCSD gives exactly twice one molecule (-0.069349 Eh). CISD gives -0.068221 Eh against -0.069349: it loses -0.71 kcal/mol, though for a single H₂ CISD and CCSD agree exactly (two electrons leave no room for a corner).
  • Two waters (6-31G): CCSD is exactly twice one water; CISD keeps 96.4% and loses 5.9 kcal/mol, more than a hydrogen bond's worth of error from two molecules that are not even touching.

The same counting runs through spin coupling: multiplicities multiply, (2s₁ + 1)(2s₂ + 1), so 2s mult-adds, and the Clebsch–Gordan series fills the product exactly (36 cases checked, and in Lean).

chemistry/01-quantum/lean/ChemQuantum.lean: core Lean 4.37.0-rc1, no mathlib, no sorry, compiled against the shared library; every theorem uses only Lean's standard axioms.

  • fragments_multAdd: (1 + cA)(1 + cB) = 1 + cA ✳ cB
  • cisd_drops_corner: what CISD drops is cAcB
  • cisd_is_weight_zero, exact_is_weight_one: the two ends of the dial
  • n_fragments: 1 + cN = (1 + c)N
  • fragments_assoc: joining N then M is joining N + M
  • three_fragments, cisd_drops_three: the three-fragment corner expansion and what CISD loses
  • cisd_gap: the N(N − 1)e₁² gap
  • cisd_not_extensive: CISD is not size-extensive for N ≥ 2
  • spin_multiplicity: (s₁+1)(s₂+1) = s₁ ✳ s₂ + 1
  • clebsch_gordan_square: the odd multiplicities of j ⊗ j fill a square
