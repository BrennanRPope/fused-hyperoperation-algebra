Lead check: not a lead

This is the clearest place the dial appears in chemistry: it is a constant enzymologists measure, and its two ends are the two standard rules for drug combinations. But all of that is established, and the dial reading predicts nothing beyond the Yonetani–Theorell equation. The rest is bookkeeping, so I didn't follow it further.

Two inhibitors: Yonetani and Theorell's α is the dial, between Loewe and Bliss

When two inhibitors I and J act on one enzyme, the four enzyme states (free, with I, with J, with both) give

v₀/vᵢ − 1 = a + b + ab/α = a ⊞ₖ b, with a = [I]/KI, b = [J]/KJ, k = 1/α

This is the Yonetani–Theorell analysis, and its interaction constant α is the dial (k = 1/α), measured routinely in enzyme kinetics. The two ends are the two standard ways pharmacologists judge drug combinations:

  • α → ∞, one shared site (k = 0): plain addition, so the 50% isobole is Loewe's straight line.
  • α = 1, independent sites (k = 1): the circle operation (called mult-add before 1.0), so the remaining activities multiply, which is Bliss independence.
  • α < 1: the bound inhibitors help each other, which is synergy.

Two side facts follow. By Loewe's yardstick independent inhibitors already look synergistic, which is part of the long Loewe versus Bliss debate. And no binding α can push the isobole beyond Loewe's line, because k = 1/α is never negative; antagonism of that kind needs another mechanism.

Michaelis–Menten is Langmuir, and steps in series add their times

Michaelis–Menten saturation has odds v/(Vmax − v) = S/Km, the Langmuir isotherm of subject 6. Irreversible steps in a catalytic cycle add their mean times, so the overall turnover rate is the reciprocal of the summed times (the k = 0 law, on 1/rate), checked by simulation.

Kinetic proofreading multiplies discrimination odds

Each proofreading stage multiplies the odds of right over wrong, so the error falls as f₀n+1: a 100 : 1 discriminator gives errors of 1%, 0.01% and 0.0001% with 0, 1 and 2 extra stages (Hopfield's scheme). In the language of subject 12 the fidelity ee composes by the velocity-addition law. The odds multiplying is the whole content; nothing further comes out.

Nitrogenase spends 16 ATP and a quarter of its electrons to do what Haber does with heat

Nitrogenase runs N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16ADP + 16Pᵢ. Six of the eight electrons reach ammonia and two go to the obligatory hydrogen, so 75% of the electrons are used. The 16 ATP supply about 488 kJ at the standard −30.5 kJ/mol, although the overall conversion of N₂ and H₂ to ammonia is downhill by 32.9 kJ. As subjects 4, 7, 8 and 9 showed, the difficulty is the barrier of the first N≡N step. Haber pays for it with heat and pressure; the enzyme pays with ATP.

Photosynthesis: the Calvin cycle stores 34% of the photons' energy at the 8-photon limit

Fixing one CO₂ takes 3 ATP and 2 NADPH, and at least 8 photons, a maximum quantum yield of 0.125. Eight 680 nm photons carry 1407 kJ per mole; one CO₂ fixed into glucose stores 2870/6 = 478 kJ, so 34% at that limit. This sits inside the stage chain of subject 10, where losses compose at k = −1 down to the 4.6% and 6.0% ceilings for whole plants.
