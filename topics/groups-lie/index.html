Every landscape law is one Möbius flow, and p² − 4q decides: a line or a circle

Each point (p, q) of the landscape is a one-dimensional group, and each has its own ruler: a logarithm that turns the law into plain addition. Drag the sliders. When p² − 4q is positive the ruler runs to infinity at two walls, and the group is a line. When it is negative the ruler closes up through the point at infinity, and the group is a circle.

Violet curve: the ruler L(x) = ∫₀ˣ ds/(1 + ps + qs²). Read a and b up to the ruler, add the two heights, read the sum back down: you land on F(a, b). Red lines are walls. Faded curve: the second piece beyond the walls.

F(a, b) = (a + b + pab)/(1 − qab)

Start at p = 1, q = 0: that is the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0), with ruler log(1 + x) and wall −1. Set p = 0, q = −1 for velocity addition (ruler artanh), p = 0, q = 1 for tangent addition (ruler arctan, a circle of length π).

The landscape is a slice of Lie's three-dimensional algebra

Every translation of a landscape law is a Möbius map: b ↦ ((1 + pa)b + a)/(−qa·b + 1), the matrix I + a·X with X = [[p, 1], [−q, 0]]. Multiplying two of them gives (1 − qab)·I + (a + b + pab)·X, which is the law up to scale. So the whole law is generated by one infinitesimal motion, the field 1 + px + qx², the Riccati rate from the landscape page.

Sophus Lie showed around 1880 that every finite-dimensional family of motions of a line is, after a change of coordinates, made of fields α + βx + γx²: three directions, translation, dilation and the projective twist. The landscape is the plane α = 1 inside that space. On it, the Killing form (the algebra's own built-in measure of a direction) is 2(p² − 4q). The border parabola q = p²/4 is the light cone of that measure.

Change the coordinate on the line by x ↦ x/(1 − cx) and (p, q) slides to (p + 2c, q + pc + c²): along a parabola, keeping p² − 4q. Rescale x and p² − 4q is multiplied by λ². So up to coordinates there are exactly three groups: a line (two walls), a line with one wall pushed to infinity (the border), and a circle (no real walls). That is why the circle operation and velocity addition, both at p² − 4q > 0, are the same group in different units. Parity, a + b − 2ab at (−2, 0), sits on velocity addition's own parabola: the chart change with c = 1 turns XOR's law into Einstein's exactly.

The lift for every point: 1 + xθ, with θ² = pθ − q

The circle operation's lift is x ↦ 1 + x. Every landscape point has one. Take a symbol θ with θ² = pθ − q and multiply:

(1 + aθ)(1 + bθ) = (1 − qab) + (a + b + pab)·θ

Divide by the plain part and the θ part is F(a, b). The rate is the size of the lift: the norm of 1 + aθ is 1 + pa + qa², and norms multiply. That one fact gives the invariant ruler: ∂F/∂b · V(b) = V(F(a, b)), so the tick spacing ds/V(s) slides along with every translation. Its integral is the logarithm L. This θ is the dial matrix U from the linear algebra page, seen as a number.

The dial is a dilation about the wall, which becomes a translation at k = 0

Read a ∘ₖ x as a motion of x: T(x) = (1 + ka)x + a. It is a straight-line map, an element of the ax + b group, and T_a ∘ T_b = T_(a ∘ₖ b). Every such motion holds one point still, the wall −1/k, and stretches the line around it by 1 + ka. The circle operation is stretching about −1.

Slide k toward 0 and the still point runs off to infinity. Stretches about a very distant point look like shifts, and at k = 0 they are shifts: plain addition, with no still point at all. Inside the ax + b algebra the generators are 1 + kx. Every k ≠ 0 is a dilation (all of them the same up to scale), and k = 0 is the translation, which no dilation can be conjugated into. That limit is a contraction in the sense of Inönü and Wigner (1953), the same move that takes Lorentz boosts to Galilean shifts as c → ∞ (q = −1/c² → 0 on the landscape).

One honest caveat: in one dimension every Lie algebra is the same line, so k is not a Lie-algebra parameter of the law itself. It sets where the wall sits and which units you use. The contraction only becomes real one level up, in the ax + b group.

On a finite clock the group is a cycle of p − 1 steps

Over the integers mod a prime p, the circle operation on everything except the wall p − 1 is a group. The lift sends it onto the nonzero residues under multiplication, which Gauss showed are a single cycle. So some element g walks through every non-wall value under repeated ∘₁ and comes home to 0 after p − 1 steps.

The simplest candidate is g = 1, and its walk is 1, 3, 7, 15, …: n copies of 1 under the circle operation are the Mersenne numbers 2ⁿ − 1. So 1 generates the clock exactly when 2 is a primitive root mod p. Mod 7 it fails (2³ = 8 = 1, so 1 ∘₁ 1 ∘₁ 1 = 0) and 2 takes over. How often 1 works for all primes is Artin's conjecture (1927), still open.

The circle groups have finite clocks too. Tangent addition on the residues mod p plus a point at infinity is a cycle of p + 1 steps when p leaves 3 over 4, and the circle x² + y² = 1 there has p + 1 points. Line, border and circle give p − 1, p and p + 1, the counts the elliptic and algebra atlas pages met as split node, cusp and turning node.

When the corner can twist, its twist is the Lie bracket

Every landscape law commutes: its matrices are all I + sX for one X. The circle operation on matrices does not. For 2×2 matrices the lift still holds, (I + A)(I + B) = I + A ∘₁ B, and the failure to commute is exactly the commutator:

A ∘₁ B − B ∘₁ A = AB − BA

For small steps, going round a little square, (I + tA)(I + tB)(I + tA)⁻¹(I + tB)⁻¹ = I + t²(AB − BA) + …, so the corner ab is where a Lie group keeps its bracket. In a group ring the same move is g ↦ g − 1: (g − 1) ∘₁ (h − 1) = gh − 1. Expanding products of 1 + x this way, the circle-operation cube, is the Magnus expansion (1937). In one dimension the corner is symmetric and the bracket vanishes, which is the whole reason every landscape law is so tame.

Lead worth following? Not on its own.

This branch gives the landscape its proper name: it is the set of one-parameter Möbius groups, and its three countries are Lie's and Klein's three kinds of motion. That makes the map trustworthy and easy to use. It predicts nothing new, because one-dimensional groups are completely classified, and the dial turns out to be a choice of units and wall position rather than a new parameter.

The one direction with real content is the twisting corner: the circle operation on matrices, where order matters and the bracket appears. In physics that is two boosts in different directions, which is being followed separately on the twisted boosts page.
