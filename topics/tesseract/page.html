Tesseract coordinates for mult-add

Every point gets 16 numbers, one for each corner of a tesseract. A corner's address says which axes it uses, so its dimension is part of its name. Mult-add is the natural way to combine these points: two corners combine by joining their switches.

The lead worth following

One tesseract, one dial. The same 16 corners show up in three very different parts of maths. The only difference is what happens when a switch meets itself.

Switch times itselfWhat you get
x·x = 0Plain addition: (1+ax)(1+bx) = 1+(a+b)x. This is the world of derivatives (dual numbers, jets).
x·x = xMult-add: (1+ax)(1+bx) = 1+(a⊕b)x. Probability, logic, solitons. This page.
e·e = ±1, order flips signGeometric algebra: rotations, and Maxwell's four equations as one (Hestenes). Spacetime algebra has exactly 16 basis pieces, in layers of 1, 4, 6, 4, 1.

Each row is known on its own. Seeing them as one dial on one tesseract is the "same lens" you were after, and it's worth chasing. It isn't a new theorem yet.

A second, smaller find. For KdV solitons, take the log of each corner weight and run the Möbius step (below). Everything lands in grade 2 and nothing in grades 3 or 4. In plain words, the coordinates show at a glance that these waves only ever interact in pairs. Checked in Python for four solitons. This is known in essence (Hirota's formula, and the same trick as cumulants in statistical physics), but the tesseract makes it visible.

The geometry underneath

Take a box with sides 1+a and 1+b and cut it at 1 on each side. It falls into four pieces: the unit square, a strip of area a, a strip of area b and a corner piece of area ab. The pieces add up to the box, so 1 + a + b + ab = (1+a)(1+b). Take away the unit square and what's left is a⊕b.

Do the same with four sides 1+a, 1+b, 1+c, 1+d and the box falls into 16 pieces: 1 unit hypercube, 4 rods, 6 slabs, 4 blocks and 1 hyperblock. Each piece sits at one tesseract corner, and its measure is the product of the letters that are switched on. This is the geometric underpinning: it's a real cut of a real box, and it holds in any number of dimensions.

1 + (a ⊕ b ⊕ c ⊕ d) = (1+a)(1+b)(1+c)(1+d) = sum of all 16 corner pieces

The definition, precisely

Corner
A set S of switches from {a, b, c, d}. Written as four digits, 1 for on.
Grade
|S|, the number of switches on. It is the corner's dimension.
Point
One weight wS for every corner: 16 numbers for n = 4, 2n in general.
From ordinary
An ordinary point (a, b, c, d) gets cS = product of its letters in S. So c0110 = bc. These are the pure points.
Back to ordinary
Read the grade-1 corners: a = c1000 / c0000, and so on.
Value at x
evx(w) = ΣS wS · Πi∈S xi. Every point is a weighted mix of the 16 corner monomials.
Box coordinates
BT = sum of cS over every corner S inside T (the zeta step). For a pure point BT = Πi∈T(1+ai), the measure of a sub-box.
Möbius step
The exact inverse: cS = ΣT⊆S (−1)|S−T| BT.
Mult-add
(v ⊛ w)T = Σ vSwU over pairs with S ∪ U = T. In box coordinates it is plain multiplication, corner by corner. For pure points it is mult-add on each axis.
As a ring
Polynomials in xa…xd with the rule xi² = xi. A switch turned on twice is still on.

Mapping back onto reality

For a pure point nothing is lost: the grade-1 corners are the ordinary coordinates, and the other 12 corners follow from them. A general point has 12 extra numbers, and those measure how far it is from being a simple product. That is where interaction lives.

You readYou getKept or lost
Grade-1 cornersThe ordinary point (a, b, c, d)Kept exactly for pure points
All 16 cornersThe point plus its interactionsKept
Box coordinatesSub-box measuresKept, the Möbius step goes back
Totals per grade1, a+b+c+d, ab+ac+…, abc+…, abcdLoses which axis is which
Sum of all corners1 + a⊕b⊕c⊕dOne number, most is lost

The one trouble spot is −1, a box side of zero. Mult-add with −1 swallows everything: (−1)⊕b = −1, just as 0×b = 0. So the information loss you dislike in ordinary multiplication moves to −1 here. It doesn't vanish.

What gets simpler, and what gets worse

Polar coordinates make circles easy and lines awkward. The same trade happens here. Every example below was checked in Python (examples.py).

SimplerAt least one of four things happens

Four independent chances: 1/10, 1/5, 1/4, 1/3.

Before: inclusion-exclusion15 terms with alternating signs, one per non-empty corner: p₁+p₂+p₃+p₄ − p₁p₂ − … + p₁p₂p₃ + … − p₁p₂p₃p₄
After: one mult-add chainP = −((−p₁) ⊕ (−p₂) ⊕ (−p₃) ⊕ (−p₄)) = 16/25

When the events depend on each other, put the chance that all of S happen on corner S. The answer is then one signed sum over the corners, a single Möbius step. Checked against a direct count of 2,000 simulated cases. Known Inclusion-exclusion is the Möbius function of exactly this lattice (Rota, 1964).

SimplerA truth table becomes a few corners

Before: 16 rowsParity of four bits: on when an odd number of inputs is on.
After: one cornerWith inputs written as ±1, parity is the single corner abcd.

Majority of three goes from 8 rows to ab + ac + bc − 2abc. The grade of a corner tells you how many inputs interact. Worse Write parity with 0/1 inputs instead and it fills all 15 non-empty corners, with weights 1, −2, 4, −8. Which switch rule you pick matters. Known Boolean function analysis (Fourier–Walsh, algebraic normal form).

SimplerFour KdV solitons

Before: 16 exponential termsτ = 1 + eη₁ + … + eη₁+η₂·A₁₂ + … + eη₁+η₂+η₃+η₄·A₁₂A₁₃A₁₄A₂₃A₂₄A₃₄, each coefficient written out.
After: one corner rulewS = product of Aij over the pairs inside S, with Aij = ((ki−kj)/(ki+kj))².

Checked with speeds k = ½, 1, 3⁄2, 2: u = 2∂²ₓ log τ solves ut + 6uux + uxxx = 0 to within 1.5×10⁻³⁶ at 40-digit precision. Switch off the interaction (every A = 1, a pure box) and the error jumps to 7.5, so it's no longer a solution. The interaction lives entirely off the pure point. Known Hirota, 1971.

WorseRotating a point

Turn (3, 4) by the angle with cos = 3/5. In polar form you just add the angle. In corner form the new ab corner is cos·sin·(a² − b²) + (cos² − sin²)·ab. It needs a² and b², and no corner holds a square. Circles (x² + y² = r²) have the same problem. Rotations and distances belong to the e·e = ±1 row of the dial, not to mult-add. The size also doubles with every axis: 2n numbers for n axes.

KnownMaxwell as one equation

In spacetime algebra, Maxwell's four equations become ∇F = J (David Hestenes, 1966). That algebra has the same 16 corners in layers of 1, 4, 6, 4, 1, but switches square to ±1 and anticommute. So this belongs to the geometric-algebra row of the dial. It isn't a mult-add result, and it's credited here to show where the dial leads.

What's known, and what's yours

  • The box split (1+a)(1+b)… = sum of corner pieces: classical.
  • Corners as the subsets of {a, b, c, d}: the Boolean lattice. The zeta and Möbius steps on it: Rota's theory of Möbius functions.
  • Combining corners by joining switches: the subset union product, used in fast algorithms.
  • The rule x² = x: idempotent algebra, the same as functions on the cube's 16 vertices. With one axis it is the split-complex numbers.
  • Weighted corner mixes: multilinear polynomials and Boolean function analysis.
  • Grade = dimension on 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 corners: Grassmann and Clifford (geometric) algebra.
  • a⊕b = a+b+ab itself: the circle operation in ring theory.

What's yours: using grade-tagged corners as a coordinate system for ordinary space, with mult-add as its native way of combining points, and the idea that one dial on the same tesseract joins derivatives, mult-add and geometric algebra. That framing could turn into something. As of today there's no new theorem here, and every piece above matches known maths.

  TesseractCoords.lean`.

  Notation: Lean already uses `⊕` for sum types and mathlib uses `⊞` for
  biproducts, so mult-add is written `✳` here, a plus and a times sign laid
  over each other. a ✳ b = a + b + a*b.

  The picture. Cut a box with sides 1+a, 1+b, 1+c, 1+d at 1 on every axis.
  It falls into 16 pieces, one per corner of the tesseract. A corner is a
  set of switches S (which axes are "on"); its piece has measure
  ∏_{i ∈ S} a i and its grade (dimension) is |S|.
-/
import Mathlib

open Finset BigOperators

namespace MultAdd

variable {R : Type*} [CommRing R]

/-! ## Mult-add on numbers -/

/-- Mult-add: one operation that adds and multiplies at once. -/
def multAdd (a b : R) : R := a + b + a * b

@[inherit_doc] infixl:65 " ✳ " => multAdd

/-- The geometric heart: mult-add is a box with sides 1 + a and 1 + b,
minus the unit square. -/
theorem one_add_multAdd (a b : R) : 1 + (a ✳ b) = (1 + a) * (1 + b) := by
  unfold multAdd; ring

theorem multAdd_comm (a b : R) : a ✳ b = b ✳ a := by
  unfold multAdd; ring

theorem multAdd_assoc (a b c : R) : (a ✳ b) ✳ c = a ✳ (b ✳ c) := by
  unfold multAdd; ring

theorem multAdd_zero (a : R) : a ✳ 0 = a := by
  unfold multAdd; ring

/-- Four numbers: the tesseract box with 16 pieces. -/
theorem one_add_multAdd₄ (a b c d : R) :
    1 + (a ✳ b ✳ c ✳ d) = (1 + a) * (1 + b) * (1 + c) * (1 + d) := by
  simp only [multAdd]; ring

/-- Mult-add of a whole list (0 is the identity). -/
def multAddList : List R → R
  | [] => 0
  | x :: xs => x ✳ multAddList xs

theorem one_add_multAddList :
    ∀ l : List R, 1 + multAddList l = (l.map (fun x => 1 + x)).prod
  | [] => by simp [multAddList]
  | x :: xs => by
      simp only [multAddList, one_add_multAdd, List.map_cons, List.prod_cons,
        one_add_multAddList xs]

/-! ## Tesseract coordinates -/

/-- A corner of the n-cube: the set of switches that are on. -/
abbrev Corner (n : ℕ) := Finset (Fin n)

/-- Tesseract coordinates: one weight for every corner. -/
abbrev Coord (n : ℕ) (R : Type*) := Corner n → R

/-- The grade of a corner is how many switches are on: its dimension
(0 = point, 1 = line, 2 = area, 3 = volume, 4 = hypervolume). -/
def grade {n : ℕ} (S : Corner n) : ℕ := S.card

/-- Corner coordinates of an ordinary point a: corner S holds ∏_{i ∈ S} a i. -/
def ofPoint {n : ℕ} (a : Fin n → R) : Coord n R := fun S => ∏ i ∈ S, a i

/-- Back to ordinary coordinates: read off the grade-1 corners. -/
def toPoint {n : ℕ} (c : Coord n R) : Fin n → R := fun i => c {i}

/-- The evaluation map: weights become one number at x. -/
def eval {n : ℕ} (x : Fin n → R) (c : Coord n R) : R :=
  ∑ S : Corner n, c S * ∏ i ∈ S, x i

/-- Box coordinates (the zeta transform): add up every corner inside T. -/
def zeta {n : ℕ} (c : Coord n R) : Coord n R := fun T => ∑ S ∈ T.powerset, c S

/-- Mult-add of two tesseract points: corners combine by joining their
switches (S ∪ U). This is the union, or OR, product on subsets. -/
def compose {n : ℕ} (v w : Coord n R) : Coord n R :=
  fun T => ∑ S : Corner n, ∑ U : Corner n, if S ∪ U = T then v S * w U else 0

/-- Hirota's N-soliton weights: corner S gets the product of the pair
interactions A i j for i < j inside S. -/
def solitonWeights {n : ℕ} (A : Fin n → Fin n → R) : Coord n R :=
  fun S => ∏ p ∈ (S ×ˢ S).filter (fun p => p.1 < p.2), A p.1 p.2

/-! ## Facts -/

/-- Nothing is lost going in and coming back out. -/
theorem toPoint_ofPoint {n : ℕ} (a : Fin n → R) : toPoint (ofPoint a) = a := by
  funext i; simp [toPoint, ofPoint]

/-- There are n choose k corners of grade k. -/
theorem card_corners_of_grade (n k : ℕ) :
    ((univ : Finset (Corner n)).filter (fun S => grade S = k)).card = n.choose k := by
  have h : (univ : Finset (Corner n)).filter (fun S => grade S = k)
      = powersetCard k (univ : Finset (Fin n)) := by
    rw [powersetCard_eq_filter, powerset_univ]; rfl
  rw [h, card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]

/-- The tesseract's layers are 1, 4, 6, 4, 1. -/
example : (powersetCard 0 (univ : Finset (Fin 4))).card = 1 := by
  rw [card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]; rfl
example : (powersetCard 1 (univ : Finset (Fin 4))).card = 4 := by
  rw [card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]; rfl
example : (powersetCard 2 (univ : Finset (Fin 4))).card = 6 := by
  rw [card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]; rfl
example : (powersetCard 3 (univ : Finset (Fin 4))).card = 4 := by
  rw [card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]; rfl
example : (powersetCard 4 (univ : Finset (Fin 4))).card = 1 := by
  rw [card_powersetCard, card_univ, Fintype.card_fin]; rfl

/-- The 16 pieces add up to the whole box: ∑_S ∏_{i ∈ S} a i = ∏ (1 + a i). -/
theorem sum_corners_ofPoint {n : ℕ} (a : Fin n → R) :
    ∑ S : Corner n, ofPoint a S = ∏ i, (1 + a i) := by
  rw [Finset.prod_one_add, powerset_univ]
  simp only [ofPoint]

/-- So the sum of all corners is 1 plus the mult-add of the coordinates. -/
theorem sum_corners_eq_one_add_multAdd {n : ℕ} (a : Fin n → R) :
    ∑ S : Corner n, ofPoint a S = 1 + multAddList (List.ofFn a) := by
  rw [sum_corners_ofPoint, one_add_multAddList, List.map_ofFn, List.prod_ofFn]
  rfl

/-- Evaluating "every weight 1" at x gives the box ∏ (1 + x i). -/
theorem eval_ones {n : ℕ} (x : Fin n → R) :
    eval x (fun _ => 1) = ∏ i, (1 + x i) := by
  rw [Finset.prod_one_add, powerset_univ]
  simp [eval]

/-- Box coordinates of a point are sub-box measures ∏_{i ∈ T} (1 + a i). -/
theorem zeta_ofPoint {n : ℕ} (a : Fin n → R) (T : Corner n) :
    zeta (ofPoint a) T = ∏ i ∈ T, (1 + a i) := by
  rw [Finset.prod_one_add]
  rfl

/-- In box coordinates, mult-add axis by axis is plain multiplication. -/
theorem zeta_ofPoint_multAdd {n : ℕ} (a b : Fin n → R) (T : Corner n) :
    zeta (ofPoint (fun i => a i ✳ b i)) T = zeta (ofPoint a) T * zeta (ofPoint b) T := by
  simp only [zeta_ofPoint, one_add_multAdd, Finset.prod_mul_distrib]

/-- With no interaction (every A i j = 1) every soliton weight is 1, so the
tau function is the pure box ∏ (1 + e^{η i}). -/
theorem solitonWeights_one {n : ℕ} :
    solitonWeights (fun _ _ => (1 : R)) = fun (_ : Corner n) => 1 := by
  funext S; simp [solitonWeights]

  coordinates. The Python helpers test it on many random points.
-/

Python helpers

tesseract.py converts between ordinary and tesseract coordinates, lists corners by grade, runs the zeta and Möbius steps, composes points with mult-add and builds soliton weights. tesseract.py
"""Tesseract coordinates for Fused Mult-Add Algebra.

A point in n dimensions gets 2**n coordinates, one per corner of the
n-cube (n = 4 is the tesseract). A corner is addressed by n switches,
one per axis: switch i is on when axis i takes part. The corner's
grade (its dimension) is how many switches are on.

Corners are stored as bitmasks: bit 0 is axis a, bit 1 is axis b, and so
on. A coordinate vector is a plain list of length 2**n, indexed by mask.

The three ideas, in code:
  from_point(a)  -> corner coordinates  c_S = product of a_i for i in S
  zeta(c)        -> box coordinates     B_T = sum of c_S over S inside T
                                             = product of (1 + a_i), i in T
  compose(v, w)  -> mult-add on corners (switches combine by OR)

Mult-add a (+) b = a + b + ab = (1+a)(1+b) - 1. In box coordinates it is
plain multiplication, corner by corner; mobius() goes back.

Everything works with ints, floats or fractions.Fraction.
"""

from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import combinations
from math import comb

AXES = "abcdefgh"


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- numbers

def multadd(x, y):
    """Mult-add of two numbers: x + y + x*y."""
    return x + y + x * y


def multadd_all(values):
    """Mult-add of a whole list, left to right (0 is the identity)."""
    total = 0
    for v in values:
        total = multadd(total, v)
    return total


def multadd_inverse(x):
    """The y with x (+) y = 0, that is -x / (1 + x). Undefined at x = -1."""
    if x == -1:
        raise ZeroDivisionError("-1 has no mult-add inverse (its box side is 0)")
    return -x / (1 + x) if isinstance(x, float) else Fraction(-x) / (1 + x)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- corners

def size(n):
    return 1 << n


def grade(mask):
    """Number of switches on = the corner's dimension."""
    return bin(mask).count("1")


def label(mask, n):
    """Switch address in axis order a b c d..., e.g. '1010' for a and c."""
    return "".join("1" if mask >> i & 1 else "0" for i in range(n))


def name(mask, n):
    """Letters of the switches that are on, e.g. 'ac'; the empty corner is '1'."""
    s = "".join(AXES[i] for i in range(n) if mask >> i & 1)
    return s or "1"


def corners_by_grade(n):
    """List of lists: corners_by_grade(n)[k] holds every corner of grade k."""
    layers = [[] for _ in range(n + 1)]
    for mask in range(size(n)):
        layers[grade(mask)].append(mask)
    return layers


def subsets(mask):
    """Every sub-corner of mask (every mask whose switches are inside it)."""
    sub = mask
    while True:
        yield sub
        if sub == 0:
            return
        sub = (sub - 1) & mask


# ------------------------------------------------- ordinary <-> tesseract

def from_point(a):
    """Corner coordinates of an ordinary point a = (a_1..a_n).

    Corner S holds the product of a_i over the switches in S: the measure
    of that piece when a box with sides 1 + a_i is cut at 1 on every axis.
    """
    n = len(a)
    c = [1] * size(n)
    for mask in range(1, size(n)):
        low = mask & -mask
        i = low.bit_length() - 1
        c[mask] = c[mask ^ low] * a[i]
    return c


def to_point(c):
    """Back to ordinary coordinates: the grade-1 corners, scaled by corner 1."""
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    if c[0] == 0:
        raise ValueError("corner 1 is 0, so there is no scale to read from")
    return [c[1 << i] / c[0] if isinstance(c[0], float) else Fraction(c[1 << i]) / c[0]
            for i in range(n)]


def is_pure(c, tol=0):
    """True when c comes from an ordinary point (every corner factors)."""
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    if c[0] == 0:
        return all(abs(x) <= tol for x in c)
    a = to_point(c)
    rebuilt = from_point(a)
    return all(abs(c[m] - c[0] * rebuilt[m]) <= tol for m in range(len(c)))


def size_to_n(length):
    n = length.bit_length() - 1
    if length != 1 << n:
        raise ValueError("a coordinate list must have length 2**n")
    return n


def evaluate(c, x):
    """Value of the coordinates at x: sum over corners of c_S times prod x_i."""
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    if len(x) != n:
        raise ValueError("x needs one value per axis")
    monomials = from_point(x)
    return sum(c[m] * monomials[m] for m in range(len(c)))


def total(c):
    """Sum of every corner. For a pure point this is 1 + (a (+) b (+) ...)."""
    return sum(c)


def grade_totals(c):
    """Sum of the corners in each grade: [point, lines, areas, volumes, ...]."""
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    out = [0] * (n + 1)
    for m, v in enumerate(c):
        out[grade(m)] += v
    return out


# ------------------------------------------------- transforms and mult-add

def zeta(c):
    """Box coordinates: B_T = sum of c_S over every S inside T."""
    b = list(c)
    n = size_to_n(len(b))
    for i in range(n):
        bit = 1 << i
        for m in range(len(b)):
            if m & bit:
                b[m] += b[m ^ bit]
    return b


def mobius(b):
    """Inverse of zeta (Mobius inversion): c_S = sum over T inside S of (-1)^|S-T| B_T."""
    c = list(b)
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    for i in range(n):
        bit = 1 << i
        for m in range(len(c)):
            if m & bit:
                c[m] -= c[m ^ bit]
    return c


def compose(v, w):
    """Mult-add of two tesseract points: corners combine by OR of switches.

    (v * w)_T = sum of v_S * w_U over every pair with S | U == T.
    Same as mobius(zeta(v) * zeta(w)) corner by corner.
    """
    if len(v) != len(w):
        raise ValueError("both points need the same number of axes")
    out = [0] * len(v)
    for s, vs in enumerate(v):
        if vs == 0:
            continue
        for u, wu in enumerate(w):
            out[s | u] += vs * wu
    return out


def compose_fast(v, w):
    """compose() through box coordinates; O(n 2^n) instead of O(4^n)."""
    return mobius([x * y for x, y in zip(zeta(v), zeta(w))])


# ------------------------------------------------- the soliton example

def soliton_tau_weights(A):
    """Hirota N-soliton corner weights: w_S = product of A[i][j], i < j in S.

    A[i][j] is the interaction between solitons i and j. With every
    A[i][j] = 1 the point is pure: the tau function factors as
    (1 + e^eta_1)(1 + e^eta_2)...
    """
    n = len(A)
    w = [1] * size(n)
    for mask in range(size(n)):
        on = [i for i in range(n) if mask >> i & 1]
        for i, j in combinations(on, 2):
            w[mask] *= A[i][j]
    return w


def describe(c):
    """Readable listing, one line per corner, grouped by grade."""
    n = size_to_n(len(c))
    lines = []
    for k, layer in enumerate(corners_by_grade(n)):
        parts = [f"{label(m, n)} {name(m, n)}={c[m]}" for m in layer]
        lines.append(f"grade {k} ({comb(n, k)}): " + ", ".join(parts))
    return "\n".join(lines)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    a = [Fraction(1, 2), 2, 3, Fraction(-1, 3)]
    c = from_point(a)
    print(describe(c))
    print("total =", total(c), "= 1 + mult-add =", 1 + multadd_all(a))

"""Where tesseract coordinates make things shorter, and where they don't.

Run: python3 examples.py   (needs sympy for the soliton check)
Every example prints a before/after and asserts they agree.
"""

import random
from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import product

import tesseract as t


def header(s):
    print("\n" + "=" * 70 + "\n" + s + "\n" + "=" * 70)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1
def example_or_of_events():
    header("1. Chance that at least one of 4 independent things happens")
    p = [Fraction(1, 10), Fraction(1, 5), Fraction(1, 4), Fraction(1, 3)]
    # Before: inclusion-exclusion, 15 alternating terms (every non-empty corner)
    terms = []
    for mask in range(1, 16):
        prod_ = 1
        for i in range(4):
            if mask >> i & 1:
                prod_ *= p[i]
        sign = 1 if t.grade(mask) % 2 else -1
        terms.append(sign * prod_)
    before = sum(terms)
    # After: one mult-add chain on -p, i.e. P = -((-p1) (+) (-p2) (+) ...)
    after = -t.multadd_all([-x for x in p])
    print(f"before: {len(terms)} signed terms, one per non-empty corner -> {before}")
    print(f"after : -( -p1 (+) -p2 (+) -p3 (+) -p4 ) -> {after}")
    assert before == after
    # Dependent events: put P(all of S happen) on corner S, one Mobius step.
    rng = random.Random(0)
    worlds = [tuple(rng.random() < 0.4 for _ in range(4)) for _ in range(2000)]
    w = [Fraction(sum(all(x[i] for i in range(4) if m >> i & 1) for x in worlds),
                  len(worlds)) for m in range(16)]
    none = sum((-1) ** t.grade(m) * w[m] for m in range(16))
    direct = Fraction(sum(any(x) for x in worlds), len(worlds))
    print(f"dependent events: 1 - signed corner sum = {1 - none} = direct count {direct}")
    assert 1 - none == direct


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2
def anf(table, n):
    """Truth table (index = input mask) -> corner weights (Mobius transform)."""
    return t.mobius(list(table))


def walsh(table, n):
    """Fourier-Walsh weights with +-1 inputs: f = sum_S f^(S) prod x_i."""
    N = 1 << n
    out = []
    for s in range(N):
        tot = 0
        for x in range(N):
            chi = (-1) ** t.grade(x & s)
            tot += table[x] * chi
        out.append(Fraction(tot, N))
    return out


def example_boolean():
    header("2. A 16-row truth table becomes a few corners")
    n = 4
    parity = [t.grade(x) % 2 for x in range(16)]
    pm = [1 - 2 * v for v in parity]          # parity as +1/-1
    W = walsh(pm, n)
    nz = {t.name(m, n): W[m] for m in range(16) if W[m]}
    print("parity of 4 bits: 16 rows -> Walsh corners", nz)
    assert nz == {"abcd": 1}
    maj3 = [1 if t.grade(x) >= 2 else 0 for x in range(8)]
    c = anf(maj3, 3)
    nz = {t.name(m, 3): c[m] for m in range(8) if c[m]}
    print("majority of 3: 8 rows -> corners", nz, "(ab+ac+bc-2abc)")
    assert nz == {"ab": 1, "ac": 1, "bc": 1, "abc": -2}
    # where the 0/1 basis is worse: parity in 0/1 corners fills everything
    c = anf(parity, 4)
    print("same parity in 0/1 corners:", {t.name(m, 4): c[m] for m in range(16) if c[m]})

def example_kdv():
    header("3. KdV solitons: 16-term formula = one corner rule")
    import sympy as sp
    x, tt = sp.symbols("x t", real=True)
    ks = [sp.Rational(1, 2), sp.Integer(1), sp.Rational(3, 2), sp.Integer(2)]
    n = len(ks)
    A = [[((ks[i] - ks[j]) / (ks[i] + ks[j])) ** 2 if i != j else 1
          for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    w = t.soliton_tau_weights(A)
    eta = [k * x - k ** 3 * tt for k in ks]
    tau = sum(w[m] * sp.exp(sum(eta[i] for i in range(n) if m >> i & 1))
              for m in range(16))
    u = 2 * sp.diff(sp.log(tau), x, 2)
    resid = sp.diff(u, tt) + 6 * u * sp.diff(u, x) + sp.diff(u, x, 3)
    f = sp.lambdify((x, tt), resid, "mpmath")
    import mpmath
    mpmath.mp.dps = 40
    pts = [(mpmath.mpf(xv), mpmath.mpf(tv)) for xv in ("-6", "-2", "0", "1.5", "4")
           for tv in ("-1", "0", "0.7")]
    worst = max(abs(f(xv, tv)) for xv, tv in pts)
    print("tau has 16 terms; corner S weight = product of A_ij over pairs in S")
    print("KdV residual u_t + 6 u u_x + u_xxx at 15 sample points: max |r| =",
          mpmath.nstr(worst, 3))
    assert worst < 1e-25
    # control: drop the interaction (every A = 1) and it is no longer a solution
    w1 = [1] * 16
    tau1 = sum(w1[m] * sp.exp(sum(eta[i] for i in range(n) if m >> i & 1))
               for m in range(16))
    u1 = 2 * sp.diff(sp.log(tau1), x, 2)
    r1 = sp.diff(u1, tt) + 6 * u1 * sp.diff(u1, x) + sp.diff(u1, x, 3)
    f1 = sp.lambdify((x, tt), r1, "mpmath")
    ctrl = max(abs(f1(xv, tv)) for xv, tv in pts)
    print("control with every A = 1 (pure box, no interaction): max |r| =",
          mpmath.nstr(ctrl, 3))
    assert ctrl > 1e-3
    # the interaction lives only in grade 2: Mobius of log-weights
    logw = [sp.log(v) for v in w]
    m = t.mobius(logw)
    by_grade = {}
    for mask in range(16):
        g = t.grade(mask)
        val = sp.nsimplify(sp.simplify(m[mask]))
        by_grade.setdefault(g, set()).add(val == 0)
    print("Mobius of log-weights is zero at grades:",
          [g for g, s in by_grade.items() if s == {True}])
    assert by_grade[3] == {True} and by_grade[4] == {True}

def example_rotation_is_worse():
    header("4. Where it gets worse: rotating a point")
    a, b = Fraction(3), Fraction(4)
    c = t.from_point([a, b])
    # rotate by the angle with cos = 3/5, sin = 4/5 (exact)
    co, si = Fraction(3, 5), Fraction(4, 5)
    a2, b2 = co * a - si * b, si * a + co * b
    c2 = t.from_point([a2, b2])
    print("corners before:", c, " after rotation:", c2)
    # The new 'ab' corner needs a^2 and b^2, which are not corners:
    rebuilt = co * si * (a * a - b * b) + (co * co - si * si) * c[3]
    assert c2[3] == rebuilt
    print("new ab = cos*sin*(a^2 - b^2) + (cos^2 - sin^2)*ab  -> needs a^2, b^2,")
    print("which no corner holds. Polar does this in one step (add the angle).")
    print("And -1 swallows everything: (-1) (+) b =", t.multadd(-1, Fraction(5)))
