Relativity's speed limit is mult-add's wall

Einstein's velocity addition, (u + v)/(1 + uv/c²), is mult-add wearing a lens. Measure a speed by its Doppler factor squared and boosts multiply; take the log and they add. The speed of light is the wall, the same way −1 is mult-add's wall.

The disguise comes off in two steps

First the lens. Send each speed u to its lift (1 + u)/(1 − u). That number is the Doppler factor squared: how much a light signal is stretched going between the two frames. Lifts multiply exactly:

(1 + u⊕v)/(1 − u⊕v) = (1 + u)/(1 − u) · (1 + v)/(1 − v)

So with d = 2u/(1 − u), velocity addition is mult-add: d(u⊕v) = d(u) + d(v) + d(u)·d(v). Speed of light u = 1 is where d blows up, and u = −1 lands on d = −1, mult-add's own wall. Anything added to light is light, as anything mult-added to −1 is −1.

Then the ruler. Rapidity φ = ½·log(lift), so the lift is e^(2φ) and d = e^(2φ) − 1: one step of the mult-add flow. On the rapidity ruler boosts add like plain numbers, and speed is tanh φ.

The geometry: a squeeze on the light cone

Turn the chart 45° so the two light lines are the axes, P = T + X and M = T − X. A boost by u stretches P by (1 + u) and shrinks M by (1 − u). It is a squeeze, and its ratio is exactly the lift. Two boosts squeeze twice, so the ratios multiply. That is mult-add's lift (1 + a)(1 + b) drawn as rectangles.

A squeeze keeps the area P·M = T² − X², so points slide along the hyperbola, and the light lines (P = 0 or M = 0) never move. The hyperbola is relativity's circle: rapidity is twice the area of the sector swept, just as an angle is twice the area of a circle's sector.

In the dial sweep this was the κ = +1 matrix [[0, 1], [1, 0]]. Its neighbours are κ = 0, the Galilean shear where speeds just add, and κ = −1, rotation, where slopes add by the tangent rule.

On the landscape and the rungs

On the landscape map, velocity addition sits at k = 0, q = −1/c². It has no corner at all. Relativity leaves the ab cell empty and bends only the next layer, a²b and ab². Its walls 1 − a²/c² = (1 − a/c)(1 + a/c) are the light cone, two real walls placed symmetrically, so it lives in the saturating country with a speed limit. Galilean addition is the same point with c → ∞, at the origin.

It is not a rung of its own. It is rung 0 (addition) seen through tanh, and rung 1 (mult-add) seen through the Doppler lens.

The one placement that is ours: the landscape's shape number for rung s is exactly s, and velocity addition has shape 0. Put the fractional rungs in their natural units (scale so the walls sit near ±1) and rung s = t² lands at (2t, t² − 1). That is the parabola drawn here, running from relativity at t = 0 to mult-add at t = 1. In raw units the walls of rung s sit at −2/(√s(1 + √s)) and 2/(√s(1 − √s)): just above plain addition a near-symmetric speed limit about 2/√s appears, which goes to infinity at addition itself. This is a third-layer match only; the landscape page showed the fractional rungs are not exact lens laws.

Is there a lead worth following?

Yes, one: velocities in different directions. Everything above is one dimension, where velocity addition commutes and is mult-add exactly. Two boosts in different directions do not combine into a boost. They give a boost plus a small rotation (the Thomas–Wigner rotation), and the order matters. That rotation is measurable: Thomas precession supplies a factor of ½ in atomic spectra.

That is the first place relativity's addition stops being commutative, so it is a real test of the fused picture: what does the corner become when it can twist? It also leads straight to a known bridge, the Lorentz group acting as Möbius maps on the sky, and every lens on the landscape is a Möbius map.

The rung placement is a neat picture for the framework but not a physics lead on its own: it predicts nothing new.

n equal kicks

Kick a ship n times with the same speed u. Its lift is the n-th power, so its speed is ((1 + u)ⁿ − (1 − u)ⁿ)/((1 + u)ⁿ + (1 − u)ⁿ). That is the mult-add power d^(⊞n) = (1 + d)ⁿ − 1 seen through the lens. Ten kicks of 0.5c give 0.99997c: it approaches light and never reaches it.
