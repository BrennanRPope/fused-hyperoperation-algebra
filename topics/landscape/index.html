Every law we've found sits on one line of a bigger map

The map's two directions come from the circle-operation cube itself: how full the corner cell ab is (your weight k), and how much the next layer of cells, a²b and ab², bends. Every point on the plane is a real law, (a + b + k·ab) / (1 − d·ab): the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0) seen through a lens whose two walls can sit anywhere. A parabola splits it into three countries. The gaps beside our line are filled by known laws we hadn't placed yet. One spot is truly empty, and the algebra says why.

    How to read the map: the cube's next layer is the second coordinate

    Your weight k and the rung height s can't be two axes, because at the first layer they are the same number: both say how full the corner square is. The next independent number the algebra gives is the third layer of the cube, the weight on a²b and ab². For the circle operation it is 0, which is why the circle operation is a polynomial. Those two weights fix every law to the cube's third layer, the way an osculating circle fixes a curve's bend at a point. And each point is exactly one law with no higher terms, the lens law above. So each dot is the lens law that matches a real law through the cube's third layer.

    The walls are the geometry. Write 1 + k·a + d·a² = (1 − a/α)(1 − a/β). The lens M(x) = (1 − x/α)/(1 − x/β) turns the law into plain multiplication, M(a ⊕ b) = M(a)·M(b). The walls α and β are where everything gets swallowed, like −1 for the circle operation. The circle operation has one wall at −1 and the other at infinity, and all of the straight line has a wall at infinity. Velocity addition has walls at ±1, the speed of light. Where the walls sit decides the country:

    Two real walls

    Below the parabola. Things saturate: a speed limit, a carrying capacity, certainty. The lens is the circle operation.

    One double wall

    On the parabola. Addition in disguise: the lens x/(1 − x/α) turns the law into plain +. A turning point, a cusp.

    No real wall

    Above the parabola. The walls are a complex pair, so the lens is a turn of a circle: slopes, waves, rotation. Your helix lives here.

    The same plane is the cubic curves with a double point. The curve y² + a₁xy = x³ + a₂x² carries exactly the law at k = −a₁, d = −a₂ (checked symbolically, item 5 of the checks). Two walls means the curve crosses itself (a node), no real wall means the double point is a lone dot (an acnode), the border is a cusp. True elliptic curves add more terms and sit one level above this plane, landing on it at the same point when you look only to the third layer.

    The five gaps, and what's in them

    filled, known

    1. The two-walled country off the line

    Logistic growth (walls at 0 and the carrying capacity), epidemics and autocatalysis, a body falling with air drag (terminal speed is the wall), and the variance of a Kalman filter. Each is a growth rate y′ = 1 + k·y + d·y², and every such rate's runs compose by exactly the lens law at (k, d).

    Test. Integrated each law and composed two runs: error 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹⁵ (gap_tests.py, part A). Logistic started at fraction y of capacity has shape s = (1 − 2y)², so at the midpoint it is velocity addition exactly, the old tanh fact. Known since Verhulst (1838), Riccati, Kalman and Bucy (1961).

    filled, known

    2. The turning country

    Slope addition, tan(α + β), sits at (0, 1). So does a wave: the log-slope ψ′/ψ of a wave where its energy is above the hill turns through blow-ups at every node. The oxygen clock from the photosynthesis page lives next door: its flash factor 0.056 + 0.888i is a complex wall pair, a turn of 86° per flash, period about 4.

    Test. Schrödinger log-slope composes by the lens law with error 10⁻¹⁶, and the country flips exactly when energy crosses the hill: above the parabola for E > V, below for E < V, on it at E = V (part B). Known: Riccati form of the wave equation, Prüfer's angle (1926).

    filled, known

    3. The border

    The cusp curve, a wave at its classical turning point, and the Euler characteristic as a Hirzebruch genus at (2, 1). Hirzebruch's χ_y family is a straight line, d = k − 1, through K-theory (the circle operation, the Todd genus) and the signature (velocity addition); it touches the border only at the Euler characteristic.

    Test. The parabola's lens turns the law into plain addition (exact). χ_y discriminant (1 − y)² + 4y = (1 + y)², zero only at y = −1. Convention from M. Albanese's notes; Hirzebruch (1966).

    filled, with a floor

    4. The rest of the straight line

    Every k ≠ 0 on the line is the circle operation in other units, k·(a ∘ₖ b) = (ka) ∘₁ (kb), so k only says where the wall sits against the quantity's natural range. k = 2 is 2× leveraged compounding. k between −1 and 0 is partial damage: a hit that removes only a fraction f sits at k = −f, its wall beyond certainty. Below −2 there is a real floor: noise measured as an error chance can't pass the parity wall, and past it nature leaves the line for complex walls instead.

    Test. Symmetric channels sit at k = −L/(L − 1), from −2 to −1; quantum depolarizing at −d²/(d² − 1) (checked for qubit and qutrit, part C). Lean proves the floor for every L ≥ 2 (channel_floor). A 3-step cycle's eigenvalues sit on the unit circle, not below −1: a turn.

    truly empty, with a reason

    5. The fractional rungs themselves

    Rung s sits at (s, s(s − 1)/4), and every dot on that curve is occupied by a lens law. But the rung itself is not: no growth law with a smooth (analytic) rate can be a fractional rung. A law from a rate y′ = g(y) has a convergent logarithm, while rung s has logarithm E−s, the −s-th iterate of eˣ − 1, whose series diverges for every non-whole s.

    Test. Exact coefficients of the ½, −½ and ⅓ iterates: their n-th roots climb 0.25 → 0.39 → 0.53 → 0.66 and keep going (part D). Baker (1958) showed eˣ − 1 has no real non-integer iterates; Erdős and Jabotinsky (1960) confirm the whole-number rungs are the only analytic ones. What a law there would have to say: it would need a rate that isn't smooth at the start, or it could only appear as a limit.

    One correction to earlier notes. The KdV collision is not on the rung curve. Its corner weight A is between addition and the circle operation, but it sits on the straight line with no bend. Its phase shift log A is exactly the change of units that turns the KdV square into the plain circle operation. And A itself comes from velocity addition: √A = (1 − r)/(1 + r) with r the ratio of the two wavenumbers, the lens of the speed-of-light law. Sine-Gordon's tanh²(half rapidity difference) is the same fact, known.

    Laws grouped by shape

    Each settlement is linked to the library theorems that state its law, and each theorem already has a shape family in the proof gallery, read off its Lean proof by fixed rules. Put the two together and the countries sort by shape. The straight line's saturation side, union of failures through parity and the channel ladder, is almost all one shape: a rectangle with a dialed corner. The circle-operation point and velocity addition are rectangles broken into corner pieces. The rungs are cubes of any size, and the half rung's lens laws are dialed hyperboxes.

    That sorting is mostly expected rather than a discovery: the line's laws are written with the circle operation a + b + k·ab, so their proofs dial a corner by construction. What it does show is that the map's countries and the proofs' shapes agree, so the shape tells you roughly where a law lives before you compute its walls. Three settlements (2× leverage, partial damage, the qutrit) have no theorem of their own yet; they come straight from the channel floor and the dial. The library's new matrix file (DialSweep) adds three links by name: its boosts sit on velocity addition, its rotations on slope addition and its shears on plain addition, each as a 2×2 matrix I + aU.

    Without units, every law looks like one rung

    Changing units slides a dot along a parabola through the origin, so the one number units can't change is the shape, s = k²/(k² − 4d). It is the rung the law looks like: rung s has shape s exactly, so the fractional rungs cover every shape once. Reading it off tells you where the walls sit.

    Known or new

    Known: every law on the map, the lens-law family (one-parameter Möbius groups), singular cubics and their groups (Silverman, chapter III), Riccati flows, Hirzebruch genera, Baker's theorem. Over the real numbers every one of these laws is plain addition after a change of coordinates, as the Fermat page said, so the map places each law as it is written in its own natural quantity.

    Possibly new as a picture: one plane holding all the earlier pages at once, with the cube's third layer as the second axis; the rung curve and the shape number s = k²/(k² − 4d) that makes every law "look like" one rung; the floor at −2 with the exit into complex walls; and gap 5 stated as a forbidden zone for analytic laws.

    Lead check. Nothing here beats the solitons as a discovery. The most promising door is the cubic one: this plane is exactly the cubic curves with a double point, and real elliptic curves sit one level up. That is the road Wiles took, and the next map would add the cube's fourth layer to see them.
