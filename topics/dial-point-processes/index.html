One dial runs fermions to bosons, and its wall decides which can exist

Points that repel (fermions), points that ignore each other (Poisson) and points that clump (bosons) are one family, the α-processes of Shirai and Takahashi. Read α as the dial k. On any region the kernel has eigenvalues μ; fold them with ✳ₖ and the chance the region is empty is the lift of that fold to the power −1/k. The wall −1/k must stay off the eigenvalues, and on the fermion side it must sit on a whole number.

The empty-region chance is the dial fold of the eigenvalues

Shirai and Takahashi's generating functional, on a region D with eigenvalues μᵢ of the kernel there:

P(no point in D) = det(I + kK)−1/k = (1 + k·(μ₁ ✳ₖ μ₂ ✳ₖ ⋯))−1/k
−log P(no point) = Σ ℓₖ(μᵢ),   ℓₖ(x) = log(1 + kx)/k

ℓₖ is the dial's logarithm, the map that turns ✳ₖ into plain +. So for empty regions every process on the dial looks like Poisson with mean Σℓₖ(μ): at k = 0 that is the expected count, at k = −1 it is larger (fermions rarely leave gaps), at k = +1 smaller (bosons leave gaps by clumping elsewhere). At k = −1 the fold itself is the chance of at least one point, the functional-analysis page's 1 − det(I − K).

Each eigenvalue is its own independent mode

The count in D is a sum of independent pieces, one per eigenvalue (Hough, Krishnapur, Peres, Virág 2006 for k = −1; Shirai–Takahashi for all k). A mode with eigenvalue μ gives:

Existence: the wall stays off the eigenvalues, and on the fermion side it sits on a whole number

For k < 0 the wall −1/k is a positive number, and a mode is a real count only if its eigenvalue is at most the wall and the wall is a whole number m: m slots, each holding one point. Anywhere else on the fermion side some probability comes out negative. So the fermion side of the dial survives only at k = −1, −1/2, −1/3, …, each a vertical stalk that ends exactly on the wall curve μ = −1/k. An eigenvalue sitting on the wall means a slot that is always full: the region is never empty and that mode's count never varies.

For k ≥ 0 the wall is negative, out of the way of every eigenvalue, so counts in a region are always fine. The whole process needs one more condition on the kernel for most k > 0 (Maunoury 2016); at k = 2/m it always exists for a real symmetric kernel (k = 1/m for a complex one), as a Cox process.

Same rule as Sugeno's measures, plus a lattice. The measure page found λ-measures normalize when the wall −1/λ lies outside the range of values [0, 1]. Here the range is the spectrum and the rule is the same. Two differences: the process may touch the wall (a sure point; Sugeno must stay strictly off it), and the fermion side is quantized to whole m, which a measure does not need.

Number variance is a straight line in k

Var N = Σ μᵢ(1 + k·μᵢ) = tr K + k·tr K²

Each mode adds its value times its lift. That product is zero at μ = 0 and again at the wall, the two places a mode's count is certain. Summed, the variance is a straight line in k through the Poisson value: fermions below it (sub-Poisson, repulsion), bosons above (super-Poisson, clumping). The second factorial moment is (Σμ)² + k·Σμ². Known (Shirai–Takahashi; the per-mode variance μ + kμ² is Morris's 1982 quadratic variance function for binomial, Poisson and negative binomial).

Superposition slides along the dial. m independent copies of the process (k, K) are exactly the process (k/m, m·K): the lifted kernel k·K is unchanged and 1/k counts copies. Underneath is the dial identity c·(a ✳ₖ b) = (c·a) ✳k/c (c·b), proved in Lean.

Tilting a mode puts it on the landscape, always on one parabola

Weight a mode's count by tN (the standard exponential tilt). Its mean moves, by the rate m′ = m(1 + km). Measure the move in the ruler x = (new mean − μ)/variance. Then tilts compose exactly by the landscape's lens law (a + b + p·ab)/(1 − q·ab) at

p = 1 + 2s,   q = s(1 + s),   s = kμ,   so p² − 4q = 1

Every mode of every dial process lands on that one parabola, inside the two-walled country. Its two walls are the empty mode and the full mode. A rare mode (s near 0) sits at mult-add, the Poisson point. A full fermion mode sits at the union of failures. A half-filled fermion mode is velocity addition with speed limit 2. Bosons climb toward the border parabola (gamma, the clumping limit) and never reach it. The units-free shape is (2F − 1)², F the mode's Fano factor, which is why particles and holes look alike.

What the bend means here: it is not a free knob. A mode's bend is fixed by its filling kμ, so the bend adds no new processes, and no dial process lives above the border, where slope addition sits. Fractional rungs: we found nothing genuine; a fermion filling can match a rung's shape, but the rung itself is not a lens law (landscape page), so we dropped it.

Lead worth following? No, this one closes

The dial reading is exact everywhere we tried, and it gives clean pictures: voids through the dial's logarithm, existence as a wall that must stay off the spectrum on a whole-number lattice, variance as value times lift, and every mode on one parabola of the landscape. But each count statement is Shirai–Takahashi or Morris, already proved. The two open questions this was meant to settle come back empty for new math: the bend is pinned by the filling kμ, so it opens no new family of processes, and the fractional rungs have no point-process meaning we could find. Nothing here predicts a count that the known theory does not already predict, so we would not spend more time on it.
