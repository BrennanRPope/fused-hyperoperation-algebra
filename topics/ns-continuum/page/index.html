Can the dimension continuum reach 3D?

No road opens. Sliding the dimension from 2 to 3 is the same dial as turning the dissipation down from 5/4 to 1: every scaling threshold is a ray from one pivot point, so moving along dimension crosses exactly the rays that moving along dissipation does.

2D is smooth for a different reason, one scaling can't see: its vortex-stretching corner is empty. In the shell-model toy that corner doesn't fade in. Any amount of it lets the cascade through; the amount only sets how fast.

What the circle operation ∘₁ (called mult-add before 1.0) does give is an exact ruler. The whole gap between what's proved and 3D Navier–Stokes is one full rung of the circle operation's flow, and every Osgood-type proof is stuck on the bottom rung.

Nothing here is as promising as the soliton lead, and nothing here touches the Millennium problem. Each claim is tagged: proved here known, restated numerical evidence speculation.

The fan: dimension and dissipation are one dial

Navier–Stokes in n dimensions with dissipation (−Δ)α is critical on the line α = (n+2)/4: there the energy bound exactly balances the nonlinearity, and on or above it the equations are proved smooth (Lions). known, restated

Every threshold that comes from scaling depends only on the ratio γ = 2α/(n+2). So each threshold is a straight ray, and all the rays pass through one point, (n, α) = (−2, 0). proved here It's simple arithmetic, but it settles the question you asked:

  • 2D Navier–Stokes (α = 1) and Lions' 3D threshold (α = 5/4) are on the same ray, the critical one.
  • 3D Navier–Stokes (α = 1) is on the same ray as 2D flow with α = 4/5.
  • So walking the dimension from 2 to 3 at α = 1 sweeps γ from 1/2 to 2/5. Walking α from 5/4 down to 1 in 3D sweeps exactly the same rays. The dimension continuum is a reparametrized dissipation continuum, and it can't get past a ray that dissipation can't.
The 2D column is not a ray. 2D is smooth for every α ≥ 0, even with no dissipation at all (2D Euler: Wolibner 1933, Yudovich 1963). That is far below its ray. The reason is structural: in planar flow the stretching term (ω·∇)u is identically zero, so vorticity is only carried around, never amplified. This is the same "square cancels" seen in the restricted Euler model on the rungs page. known, restated the zero is proved here

Fractional dimension doesn't carry 2D's protection upward. The standard way to put Navier–Stokes in a non-integer dimension D is fractal Fourier decimation (Frisch, Pomyalov, Procaccia, Ray 2012): keep a random set of modes whose count grows like kD. It keeps the parent equation's invariants, and it can only lower the dimension. So D between 2 and 3 means decimated 3D flow, which has energy and helicity but no enstrophy, and no known smoothness below Lions' ray. known, restated

The shell is the circle-operation cube

Where does the n in α = (n+2)/4 come from? From counting cells. Doubling an n-dimensional box of side 2j adds 2nj·(2n − 1) new cells, and 2n − 1 is exactly 1 combined by the circle operation with itself n times: 1 ∘₁ 1 ∘₁ … ∘₁ 1. proved here (Lean, unchecked)

The new cells come graded by the cube's layers: C(n, k) pieces where k of the n coordinates landed in the upper half. In 2D that's two strips and one corner. proved here (Lean, unchecked)

The nonlinearity's strength in a shell goes like one derivative times the square root of the shell's cell count (Bernstein's inequality, as in Katz–Pavlović). That gives a growth factor 21+n/2 per shell against the dissipation's 22α, and the critical line is 2α = 1 + n/2. known, restated

The pivot n = −2 is where the coupling's growth factor is 1: its circle-operation count is 0, the identity. That's the one geometric fact the fan has, and it is bookkeeping, not a new mechanism.

The ruler: the gap is one full rung wide

Write the dissipation as s5/2/D(s) at frequency s, where D is the shortfall from Lions' power. Three facts are known: known, restated

  • Tao (2009) proved 3D smoothness when D is as large as (log s)1/4, more exactly whenever ∫ ds / (s·D4) diverges.
  • He conjectured the weaker test ∫ ds/(s·D2) = ∞, so up to (log s)1/2. Barbato, Morandin and Romito proved that for the dyadic model in 2014; for real Navier–Stokes it's open.
  • True 3D Navier–Stokes needs D = s1/2.

Both tests are Osgood tests, the same test that sorted the rung flows on the earlier page: a growth law lives forever exactly when its Osgood integral diverges. Here it sorts dissipations instead of growth laws.

Now measure each D by how many rungs of the circle operation's flow Et (E(x) = ex − 1) it sits above log s. Powers of log s are height 0. s1/2 − 1 = E(½ log s) is height 1. That height is a known idea (the "exponentiality" of a function in Hardy-field and transseries theory), so the ruler is standard. Putting the Navier–Stokes gap on it gives a clean picture:

Everything proved or conjectured sits at height 0. 3D Navier–Stokes sits at height 1. No Osgood-type test can leave height 0: any D that beats every power of log s makes ∫ d(log s)/Dp converge, for any fixed p. proved here (on paper)

The fractional rungs fill the gap with one ordered family, Dt(s) = Et(½ log s). At t = 0 it's ½ log s, and at t = 1 it's s1/2 − 1, which is exactly 3D Navier–Stokes. Each Dt with 0 < t < 1 grows faster than every power of log s and slower than every power of s, so all of them sit strictly inside the gap. The classical in-between family exp((log s)β) all sit at height 1, as high as Navier–Stokes itself by this ruler. known, restated (half-exponential bounds) checked numerically to s = 10300

What this does and doesn't buy. It turns "close the gap" into a ladder of intermediate problems: is Navier–Stokes with deficiency E1/2(½ log s) smooth? A proof at any positive height would be a supercritical result beyond every known log improvement. Nothing here gives one, and I see no circle-operation mechanism that would. speculation One caveat from the checks: the asymptotic order between exp((log s)1/2) and the half rung only shows past s = 10300, so the heights are an asymptotic statement, not a visible one. 14 of 14 checks pass (rung_height.py).

Dyadic model: sliding the dimension, it breaks exactly at n = 4

The Katz–Pavlović dyadic model keeps one number per shell and the Bernstein scaling above, so it takes any real dimension n. I ran it at α = 1 for n from 2 to 6 and watched how deep the energy cascade got as I added shells. If the deepest shell stops growing, the cascade is held. If it keeps up with the number of shells, energy is reaching infinitely small scales in finite time. numerical evidence

Known so far: blow-up below γ = 1/3 (Cheskidov 2008), smooth from γ = 2/5 up (Barbato–Morandin–Romito 2011). 3D sits exactly on the 2/5 line, so the dyadic model is proved smooth in 3D and can't speak for the real equations. The band 1/3 ≤ γ < 2/5 was open as of the 2022 survey. known, restated

The numerics put the real break at γ = 1/3, which is n = 4 at α = 1: the constant-energy-flux (Kolmogorov) line. Everything in the open band looked smooth. Nothing special happens at n = 2 or n = 3; the dimension continuum is featureless here too.

The corner dial: does 2D's protection fade or switch?

2D is smooth because its stretching term is zero, not because of scaling. A helical shell model (Benzi and others, 1996) lets you turn that term up from zero: the cross-handed triad corners carry a weight ε, and ε = 0 is the 2D-like corner where those triads are switched off. I swept ε and the dissipation power α to see whether the protection fades out gradually or switches off at once. numerical evidence

It switches. At ε = 0 the cascade never reached the top shell anywhere I tried. For every ε above zero, the cascade got through at small α, and the reach/held edge sat between α = 0.5 and 0.75 for ε = 4 and for cross-handed-only. Smaller ε only slowed it: at ε = 0.25 the arrival time grew from under 2 to between 3 and 16, but the pattern across α stayed the same.

The ε = 0.05 cell shows the trap. With viscosity 0.001 it held through t = 400. With viscosity 0.00001 it reached at t ≈ 96, and with none at t ≈ 93. So weak coupling doesn't protect. It slows the cascade until viscosity at that data size can drain the energy first. That is a size-of-data effect, not a change in the critical exponent.

Gaps: ε = 0.25 above α = 0.3, ε = 1 above α = 0.2, and ε = 0.1 weren't finished. The mixed cells (ε = 1 at α = 0.2, cross-handed-only at α = 0.5) reached at 24 shells but not within t = 40 at 32 or 40, which reads as timing, not protection. For the dimension question the upshot is the same as the fan: there's no partial 2D protection to slide along toward 3D. You either have the empty corner or you don't.
